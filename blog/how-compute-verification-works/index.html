Slow version. Four parts: why synchronous training needs bandwidth, what DiLoCo changes, then your two brainstorms (compression, architecture) each of which is a real research line, and finally what they stack to.

Why synchronous data-parallel training is bandwidth-bound

Every worker holds a full copy of the parameters θ. Each step, worker i takes its shard of the batch, computes a gradient g_i, and then all workers all-reduce: every g_i is summed and everyone ends up with the same averaged gradient, applies the same Adam update, and stays in lockstep. The copies never differ.

The cost is that the full gradient crosses the network every step. In a ring all-reduce each node sends and receives about 2× the gradient size, so with bf16 gradients that is 4N bytes per step for N parameters. For a 1T-parameter model that is 4 TB per step. If your per-node batch is 8k tokens on 8 H100s, a step takes about 12 seconds, so you need 330 GB/s per node just to keep up. That is why the fast fabric exists: 8 ports of 400G InfiniBand is 400 GB/s. Take the fabric away and a step waits minutes on comms for every 12 seconds of compute.

Everything below attacks the “every step” or the “full gradient” part.

DiLoCo

Local SGD is old (federated averaging, McMahan et al. 2017; Stich 2018): let each worker run H steps on its own data with no communication, then average the parameters. The problem is that after H steps the copies have drifted, and naive averaging of drifted models gets worse as H grows. In practice people could get H of maybe 4 to 16 before quality fell off.

DiLoCo (Douillard et al., DeepMind, 2023) makes H ≈ 500 work with almost no loss. The trick is to treat the drift as a gradient and put an optimizer on it.

  1. All workers start from the same θ.
  2. Each worker runs H steps of ordinary AdamW on its ownlt θ_i. This is the inner optimizer. No communication.Each worker computes its delta Δ_i = θ − θ_i. This is or pseudo-gradient. Average the Δ_i across workers. Thisis the one all-reduce.
  3. Apply an outer optimizer to the global parameters usi were a gradient. DiLoCo uses Nesterov momentum with alarge learning rate, around 0.7.
  4. Broadcast the new θ, go to 2.

The outer Nesterov momentum is what makes it work. Plain averaging (outer lr = 1, no momentum) is federated averaging and degrades with H. Momentum on the pseudo-gradient smooths the sequence of averaged deltas across outer steps and lets the global trajectory move faster than any single averaging step would. The intuition for why averaging drifted workers is valid at all is linear mode connectivity: workers that start from the same point and take a few hundred steps she loss landscape, and the loss along the straight linebetween them is low. Model merging works for the same reason.

Communication per step drops from 4N to 4N/H. At H = 500 also tolerates stragglers and dropouts, because the outer step just averages whoever showed up.
