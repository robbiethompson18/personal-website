Pacing

★★☆☆☆

Published November 17, 2026Last edited November 17, 2026

I am bullish on AI labs establishing best practices which incidentally slow down the pace of progress. These mostly serve the purpose of slowing down now, while Verified Compute Allocation Disclosures (VCAD) handle slowing down later.

Going Slower Now #

All numbers estimate quickly. You could convince me they are off by an OOM.

Security Measures (eg SL4) That, as a Side Effect, Slow Down Research Progress #

The SF rumor mill tells me this would slow down research progress by tens of percentage points. It’s not clear how to operationalize this. Lab employees could insist that weights be under SL4 level security in nnn months. The labs take security very seriously, yet still not seriously enough. Further reading: LessWrong, Situational Awareness.

External Audits Prior to Internal Deployments #

Many experts believe that most takeover risk arises from internal deployments at labs, not rogue agents escaped from the datacenter. Yet most safety and auditing work goes into model cards which inform and block external deployments. For Mythos Preview, Anthropic did only a 24-hour internal check before deploying internally. Their RSP requires a Risk Report 30 days after internal deployment.

I don’t expect labs to let their compute sit idle, and there are more VCAD-flavored compute uses which I expect to be helpful. Starting with ideas I have not seen proposed seriously:

Spend 5% of Compute Searching for Vulnerabilities in Open Source Software #

AI labs are already doing this (see Daybreak, Glasswing). Claude estimates⁠[1] that O(30bps)O(30\text{bps})O(30bps) of Anthropic’s compute went to Glasswing; it’s not clear how much more compute these projects could absorb.

Spend 3% of Compute on Monitoring of Internal or External Deployments #

AI labs reveal a surprisingly large amount about their monitoring setups.⁠[2] Claude Fermi estimates that O(30bps)O(30\text{bps})O(30bps) of frontier lab compute goes to monitoring.⁠[3] Given Hugging Face, the real number can and should be much higher.

Spend 80% of Compute on External Inference #

This proposal is the most boring, most scalable, easiest to operationalize, and does the most work towards building robust ways of validating where compute gets spent. The hard part is the how, which I discuss briefly in the next section.

Going Slower in the Future #

Much ink has been spilled on “how to prove that you are not spending your compute on AI R&D.” AI 2040 is a great place to start.

I plan to write about this in more detail in the future. Here is my teaser of promising approaches:

  • Use hardware guarantees to ensure you are running the model you say you are (slightly immature).
  • Use zk-snarks to sign inference, enabling privacy while guaranteeing that your tokens came from a fixed model. This is 2-3 OOMs too slow to be useful at present.
  • Make your whole datacenter open and public, then recompute random outputs to guarantee that a fixed model is doing inference. This assumes that “open and public” isn’t catastrophic, which is a big if.
  • Physically destroy horizontal bandwidth inside your datacenter. This makes large training runs hard but not impossible and doesn’t make much sense.
