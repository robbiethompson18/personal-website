Enunciation

Published November 11, 2026Last edited November 11, 2026

My sister told me last week that my pronunciation changed. I speak clearly and emphasize all my consonants now. This is a huge win! But I’m disconcerted; I spent years trying to stop mumbling, and suddenly at 26 I quit by accident?

Child Speak #

I grew up with four sisters. Our seven person family ate dinner together every night. Everyone is smart and opinionated. My dad liked to be intellectually provocative. I got most of my words in while he chewed. I blame my fast mumbling speech on this.⁠[1]

Trader Speak #

The most salient part of the Jane Street Dialect is words for designating responsibility. When a large quote lands, the first thing you’ll hear shouted is “MINE” or “YOURS.” Almost all statements get an immediate response like “ack” or “agree” or “weakly disagree.” Jane Street spends a lot of time teaching interns how to use trader lingo properly (“I’m 5 at 8, 7 up”), but this responsibility-delegation language is more important and needs no purposeful teaching.

An infuriating experience from other workplaces: someone will say or Slack “we should really do x,” others express agreement, and … the conversation dies. People think I’m intense when I reply with “yours Bob” or “mine,” but I refuse to unlearn this habit.

Rat Speak #

I pronounce 't’s now! Instead of saying “I dunno” or “lil” I say “I don’t know” and “little.” This is downstream of spending more time with EAs and rationalists. I spent a while digging into where their consonant overpronunciation originates and could not find a satisfying answer.

Math Speak #

Both Jane Streeters and Rationalists use math jargon in everyday speech in ways that enrich conversation:

  • The last Lyft bike is adverse.
  • The match was going poorly, I wanted vol.
  • Yes I’m attracted to your intellect, it’s hard to say what percentage, because my crush on you is so overdetermined.⁠[2]
  • Amongst my friends he is on the Pareto frontier of “lives close to me” and “strong” so I asked him to help move the piano.
  • The invite is a free option.

Increasingly I find it hard to communicate with people who do not understand statements like the above, which is problematic.

But I am guilty of using nerdspeak needlessly, which I find grating when done by others. Examples with normal alternatives in parens:

  • The two ideas are orthogonal (unrelated).
  • I eat two meals a day in expectation (on average).
  • Taking e-bikes costs epsilon dollars (is cheap).
  • It costs approximately (about) $20.

Qualifiers #

I used to see superfluous qualifiers, like “I propose that pickleball is tennis for narps” or “I suspect that Chan will lose the election,” as nerd-signaling. In reality this is nerd-signals-deference.⁠[3] To signal uncertainty, one says “80% Chan loses the election.” To signal deference, one says “I suspect she will,” with the implication “it is my smooth smooth brain doing the suspecting, you should trust your own intellect over mine.” I sure did use a lot of these qualifiers as a new hire at Jane Street.

  2. Yes, I really said this to a woman, and she thought this was a perfectly normal thing to say, and my crush became that much more overdetermined. ↩︎
