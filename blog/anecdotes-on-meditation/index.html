Meditation Anecdotes

★★★☆☆

Published November 18, 2026Last edited November 18, 2026

Meditation was a big part of my life for many years. I did a ten day silent Vipassana retreat, TAed a mindfulness course, led many a friend through guided meditations, and practiced for two to six hours a week for months at a time.

At this point in my life I almost never meditate outside of long car and plane rides. If I need to relax, taking a long walk in the park without my phone or doing yoga works better. If I need to make an emotionally fraught decision, journaling works better.

I can’t recommend a practice which I have spent hundreds of hours on. Possibly my mind is forever altered in ways I don’t appreciate, and I am being unfair to meditation.

Every meditation teacher I have ever had has made the practice about self-realization or personal improvement. None of them have encouraged me to revel in the pleasant sensations of meditation. This is silly. Relishing pleasant sensations deepens my meditative experience, spurs me to meditate more frequently, and is literal joy in and of itself.

The folks at Jhourney⁠[1] agree. They teach meditation purely to allow practitioners to access the jhanas: “meditative experiences from full body bliss to deep peace.” Self-realization be damned. It’s possible they’ll convince me to pay them $5000 for a retreat in February, despite everything I wrote above.

It’s hard not to mention the analogy between Jhourney and wireheading. Good thing I bite that bullet.

Some anecdotes from my meditation journey thus far:

The Good #

The only practical skill which meditation helped me with is breathholding. Hypoxia evokes dependable physical responses in my body: tingling in the extremities, then numb extremities, then pressure in my chest as my diaphragm relaxes, then increased blood pressure in my head, then a swallowing motion in my throat, which increases in frequency until my pharynx stays contracted continuously. The first time I experienced any of these sensations I said to myself “that is new and weird, I am afraid, I will breathe now and make sure I’m not harming myself.” Once I experienced tingling a couple times, I got comfortable and held until the numbness, and so on. Now I can sit perfectly relaxed through the first several symptoms, knowing that I am in familiar territory until my pharynx stays clenched.

Holding your breath isn’t that fun, but freediving sure as hell is. Because I could already hold my breath for four minutes, I dove to 80 feet on my fourth day of learning.

If I sit still enough in my meditation, my heartbeat is unmissable. My heart pounds inside my chest, even when I’m relaxed. Once I notice it, its beats will keep me company all day.

One of the best meditation experiences I ever had was massaging my face half an hour into a sit. My facial muscles were so relaxed. No one goes to the gym and stretches their face; rarely do we notice the tension we hold in it.

Gently exiting a long sit with a slow meal feels intense and pleasurable. The sky is bluer, flowers smell more fragrant, touching trees and kissing their bark feels divine.

The Bad #

On the fourth day of my Vipassana retreat they introduced sittings “of strong determination.” You are instructed to not move for the two hours. I missed the part where they said “if you are in pain you should move.” My knees hurt so badly that I literally cried in pain, tears streaming down my face. I didn’t sob though; I couldn’t move! Twenty minutes into this I experienced exactly what one “should”: the pain became totally divorced from my affect. I was not upset or having a bad time. I was just in pain.

On the eighth day of my Vipassana retreat my mind kept spinning in what felt like tauntingly stupid circles. It couldn’t stop reciting the lyrics to Tyler Herro, except to remember and long for the two prettiest women I had ever kissed. We weren’t allowed reading materials or a journal. In a bout of insanity, my mind attempted to regain homeostasis by opening up my alarm clock, taking the batteries out, and reading their warning labels. In every language. Thrice. This righted my mind. I sheepishly put the batteries back, went to sleep, and sang Tyler Herro in my head for two more days before going home.

The Ugly #

Once while visiting the meditation class I used to TA, I stood up during a meditation, because my leg was cramping. Five minutes later I fainted, crashing to the floor and interrupting the rest of the class’ sit. I was fine.
