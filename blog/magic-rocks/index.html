Godric got into the flying carriage and headed back to his castle. He took his crystal ball from his pantaloons, shook it, and began chatting with his mother. She asked him about the new girl he was seeing.

Just before arriving at home, he asked his crystal ball for dinner. Salmon from the Nothern Seas, the freshest fruits of the New World, and ice cream with the finest cinnamon of Ceylon for desert. Godric never did like the grown platterware, so he used his China, which had been delivered last week from a galleon sailing from the Amazon.

Godric spoke into his crystal ball: “squire Claudian, are there any messages awaiting me?”

“One, sir. Master Carson asks what you think of his decree.”

“I shall wait to respond until the morning. Now bring in my jester.”

The jester produced three cats from his sleeves, each more adorable than the last. Godric watched them chase laser pointers for a while, before tiring. Just in time, his mistress entered.

Maud was born with brown hair and a sunken chest. But she had an old witch in the forest cast a spell of beauty on her. Now her hair shone brighter than the sun, and on Sundays many townsfolk lost track of the sermon while admiring her bosom. She used jojoba from the Sahel on her skin, and it glistened like the moon.

Maud was not Godric’s only mistress. His Apothecary had warned him of the ill humors he might accumlate from his many affairs. So Godric wrapped his wand in a wizard’s sleeve before enjoying Maud’s company.

Afterwards, the two had their servants fetch wood from the shed to heat them water for a steaming bath. They dried themselves with soft cotton sheets from the Andes.

“Squire Claudian, awaken us at 7 and 20 minutes past midnight. I must get up early tomorrow for my meeting with Master Carson; the bridge will be crowded with flying carriages.”
