Magic Rocks

★☆☆☆☆

Published November 19, 2026Last edited November 19, 2026

Godric got into the flying carriage and headed back to his castle. He took his crystal ball from his pantaloons, shook it, and began chatting with his mother. They talked face to face, though she lived a month’s ride away.

Godric’s mother asked him about the new girl he was seeing. Godric tired of this conversation, bid his mother good day, and donned his magic earwax. The voices of a whole cathedral of young eunuchs filled his eardrums. He closed his eyes to enjoy the remainder of his journey.

Just before arriving at home, he asked his crystal ball for dinner. Salmon from the Northern Seas, the freshest fruits of the New World, and ice cream with the finest cinnamon of Ceylon for dessert.

While awaiting his feast, Godric lifted the heavy stones in his salon, one at a time. Once he had lifted all twenty-seven, he strode once around his garden, then sat back in the salon to catch his breath.

Godric ate his dinner by candlelight. He never did like cheap alchemical platterware, so he used his china, which had been delivered last week from a galleon sailing from the Amazon. He filled only the finest crystal chalices when drinking from the spring in his kitchen.

Godric spoke into his crystal ball: “Squire Claudian, are there any messages awaiting me?”

“One, sir. Master Carson asks what you think of his decree.”

“I shall wait to respond until the morning. Now bring in my jester.”

The jester produced three cats from his sleeves, each more adorable than the last. Godric watched them chase laser pointers for a while, before tiring. Just in time, his mistress entered.

Maud was born with brown hair and a sunken chest. But she had an old witch in the forest cast a spell of beauty on her. Now her hair shone brighter than the sun, and on Sundays many townsfolk lost track of the sermon while admiring her bosom. She used shea butter from the Sahel on her skin, and it glistened like the moon.

The townsfolk had been cruel to Maud, because of her transformation and because she was a carter. They expected her to work as a seamstress, or some other more ladylike profession. The old witch took pity on her, and gave her a fungus from the refuse of a cow. Maud swallowed her pride, swallowed the fungus, and entered a trance. After some hours, she reentered reality. The ire of the townsfolk ceased to bother her.

Maud was not Godric’s only mistress. His apothecary had warned him of the ill humors he might accumulate from his many affairs. So Godric wrapped his wand in a wizard’s sleeve before enjoying Maud’s company.

Afterwards, the two had their servants fetch wood from the shed to heat them water for a steaming bath. They dried themselves with soft cotton sheets from the Andes.

“Squire Claudian, awaken us at 7 and 20 minutes past midnight. I must get up early tomorrow for my meeting with Master Carson; the bridge will be crowded with flying carriages.”
