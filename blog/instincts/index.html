Instincts

★★★☆☆

Published November 24, 2026Last edited November 24, 2026

When possible, follow bodily instincts:

I am outing myself, but: I have no routine for how often I brush my teeth. When I’m drinking sugary drinks it’s twice a day at least. But if I haven’t eaten or mouthbreathed⁠[1] all day and I don’t have a date, I probably won’t brush.

The same goes for showering. And I rarely feel the need to shower after exercise. I enjoy having a bit of sweat dried onto my skin; the oil feels protective. I shower when I smell bad, which is driven by stress-sweat. Or when I have work or a date.

I certainly do not have a skin-care routine, and I only wear sunscreen in places further south than the Bay Area, but if I do spend enough time outside my dry skin craves some argan oil.

I make no effort to meditate beyond my natural craving for it. Whenever my emotions are sufficiently intense, I close my eyes and sit with them. When I’m in the car I sometimes relish the chance to sit in peace.

My first job out of college I worked 55 hours a week, but it was on a strict schedule I did not set. I felt tired constantly.

Since then I’ve been able to set my own schedule, to nap for half an hour after lunch and take a walk whenever I please. I can easily work 70 hour weeks and feel energized throughout.

Never in my life have I routinely woken up to an alarm. If I’m busy or need to be up early, stress will wake me. I still set the alarms, to put my mind at ease.

I have tried so many diets. This was worth doing to learn how my body reacts to things. But at this point I eat almost entirely based on what I crave (and is ethical). I eat almost all of my calories between noon and 6pm, have plenty of sugar while I’m working, and eat little or nothing if I’m sufficiently stressed, busy, or anxious. I make up for this once the anxiety passes, don’t worry.

And I consume an absurd amount of Maldon salt.

I don’t force myself to exercise. I just wake up and want to run, or do yoga or calisthenics.

Notably, this was not always the case. Running was unpleasant back when I wore shoes, and OK when I was a mouthbreather, and now it is delightful.

Likewise for strength training: as a high schooler I forced myself to work out for the sake of looking hot. Nowadays my body craves a lift when I’ve been idle and craves a stretch most days. I have no routine. If my hip flexors are tight I stretch them; if not, not.

I got here by spending hundreds of hours on weightlifting YouTube as a teenager, and hundreds of hours learning yoga in my twenties. But now I instinctually match sensation to the movement I need in response.

When my life is busy and stressful, I don’t have the energy to exercise outside of walking, so that is what I do. If the stressful episode lasts too long, I go to the sauna and get a full cardio workout by just sitting.

I have stumbled upon a lot of ‘science’ by following bodily intuitions:

  • Brushing after soda feels bad: the acid weakens enamel.
  • Your body releases ‘apocrine’ sweat in response to adrenaline. Only this type smells (eventually, after bacteria eat the protein within). High temperature instead triggers ‘eccrine’ sweat, which is odorless (until it leads to mildew).
  • Digesting takes a lot of energy; your body doesn’t want to do it while you’re stressed.

We should be sensation-maxxing more. I don’t have a good scientific explanation for why almost-fainting feels so good, but I do it anyway; I’m sure a scientist will explain this soon-to-be hot new health fad someday soon.

Notes #

  1. Mouthbreathing dries out your saliva, which makes the mouth more acidic and thus harms enamel. ↩︎
