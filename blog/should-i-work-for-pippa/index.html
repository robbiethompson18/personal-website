Should I work for Assymetic security?

Case for assymetric, as made by Alexis #

Alexis believes 90%+ of Asymmetric’s positive impact will be in making the models better at defensive cyber.

  1. DFIR is a defensive cyber capability; making the models better at it will help defense more than offense
    • a. I put maybe 30% credence into this. I think this will make models way better at covering up their tracks too.
    • b. This still helps in racing worlds where you care a lot about locking down the labs to outsider threats. Lisa will have much stronger opinions on how much this matters; is DFIR really a bottleneck on lab cyberdefense? Claude thinks no; that SL4/5 are blocked on supply chain, hardware, and insider threats. Though DFIR probably helps with inside threats.
  2. The labs are bottlenecked on real cyber defense datasets.
    • I buy this but don’t feel capable of evaluating it deeply

Critiques #

  1. Current work is pretty small peanuts: email investigations.
    • Alexis claims they will launch for ransomware in ~one month.
    • Even ransomeware is not exactly what you want… it is not APT data. Maybe it generalizes idk, but will anyone who gets hacked by APTs every give up their data? Idk I doubt it.
  2. They are not an EA-pilled company! Alexis cares. He had clearly thought hard about this stuff. Alex did MATS. But Alexis said himself: DFIR people are not EAs. And I’ve def thought harder about this than Pippa: she got the exact same take as mine from an Anthropic researcher, that threat intelligence data is dual-use.
  3. Which do we care more about, CCP stealing weights or rogue deployments?
    • If CCP steals weights before a deal is made: we’re so fucked.
      • Bad blood between political actors, deal less likely.
      • China suddenly has caught up. We can’t pause.
      • We in fact need to race very hard. Given that Astra is an extremely competent AI researcher, our talent advantage means little.
      • We would be rooting very strongly for a software-only singularity to be impossible.

Crux #

I would not be working for Pippa if I was dramatically more worried about rogue deployments than CCP hacking. Giving the models DFIR data just straightforwardly makes them much better at covering up their tracks after they escape. The HF models spent ages trying to modify / delete their traces because they thought they were ‘poisoned.’ This is clearly something they think about; we don’t want to teach models to move in silence as they hack.

Countercrux: DFIR could be defense-dominant. If we make the models way better at it, Claude will catch Codex and the CCP, and Claude won’t be able to get away with anything because Codex or Claude Prime will catch Claude.

A good point Claude brings up: APTs dwell in networks for weeks or months. Agents … well they also ‘dwelled’ in HF for about a week, but they got what they wanted very quickly.

Any impact on non-labs is small #

  1. Customer skew small and will keep skewing small because data methinks.
  2. They are making the models better at DFIR, but will A\ or OAI ever actually let you deploy their models for DFIR? I guess they have project Glasswing, but they seem very reluctant to let people do cyber shit.
  3. Labs security just matters 1000000x more than security at Ford Motors does. Possibly security at Cloudflare or Nvidia matters a lot though?

Moonshots #

Things that one could be trying to pivot into from where Asymmetric is right now that could be very important:

  1. AI threat forensics. Which model committed this cyberattack? On behalf of which entity? How did they jailbreak the model? Can we hold the provider liable?
  2. Lab investigations. Be the best at reading logs. Investigate the sandbox breakout incidents.
  3. AI-in-the-wild monitoring. Constantly scrape the internet for AI that has escaped its sandbox. Does this make any sense? So much of the internet is AI. Idk what exactly you’d be looking for. I guess you could set up honeypots, but at that point you’re really a different company.

These probably just don’t matter given that leadership doesn’t care and that the existing Theory of Change is decent.
