Everything Speeds Up The Race

Published August 27, 2026Last edited August 27, 2026

If you zoom out enough, almost all research is liable to speed up timelines and improve capabilities. RLHF was intended for alignment but made ChatGPT a commercial success. Improving reward hacking makes Claude better at coding and thus speeds up RSI. Improved bio and cyber classifiers allow Anthropic to roll out Fable widely, earn more revenue, and grow faster. Even scenario-building like AI 2027 draws attention to the field and intensify the perception of a race. Every lab started out with a mission of the flavor “make AI go well.” Now plausibly our best intervention is to make them all pause.

Lots of AI safety people are focused on ‘how can we safey get models to do alignment research.’ If they can do alignment research, they can do capabilities research.

All things considered, this argument makes me nervous. One wonders if Alex Krizhevsky was the most effective altruist.

Some hazy counterarguments:

  • Humans have some Pareto frontier they are willing to walk, with the axes being (how aligned the models seems, how powerful the models are). Moving this frontier to be more conservative is strictly good. Improving alignment faster means that, if are frontier was bad and we were willing to create a superintelligence that will kill us, we will do so faster. When we go extinct is pretty inconsequential relative to if we go extinct.⁠[1]
  • Race dynamics are impossible to change. Capabilities will proceed at a predetermined pace, but you can choose to speed up alignment. I somewhat buy this.

I don’t know how hard to update on the fact that there are countless researchers at the big lab who seem not to be worried about AI x-risk. Being worried is correlated with having thought hard about x-risk, but the causal arrow points in both directions.

If you take this post to its logical conclusion, and you buy the premise that AI x-risk is real, the logical conclusion is to go to DC and work on regulation and diplomacy. I not going to do this, because

  1. I don’t have that much conviction in my own beliefs.
  2. I don’t think I’d be great at politics.
  3. I don’t want to be live in DC.

I hope I am not letting (3) have a bigger impact than I realize.

Notes #

  1. This is a woefully oversimplified model and doesn’t account for countless other non-IABIED risks (eg bioterrorism, concentration of power, war). ↩︎
