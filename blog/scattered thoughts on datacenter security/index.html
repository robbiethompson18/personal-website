scattered thoughts on datacenter security

★★☆☆☆

Published November 22, 2026Last edited November 22, 2026

Epistemic status: I don’t work at a lab, and I don’t have close friends who do, and I’ve never done large (>x flops) training runs, but I have spent ~200 hours of my life thinking about these topics. So take all numbers and claims in this post as the best guess of an informed amature armed with lots of AI help.

The AI Safety community is correctly freaking out about security at frontier AI labs. If we can’t make a deal with China to pause, and the US pauses unilaterally, and then China steals model weights, then we are forced to race again to not fall behind.

Here are some questions I hope to deconfuse myself about:

  • The world seems to believe that frontier labs are thoroughly infilitrated, both in cyberspace and by employees who are MSS.⁠[1] Yet the labs seem to care a lot about security.
  • There are many things the CCP could steal: weights, algorithms, RL environments, RL rollouts, source code, etc. What is important? What is likely to go?
  • How to model the CCP? Asleep at the wheel? Very wise to AGI and acting clueless so that they can steal the weights at the right moment?
  • what things are easy to steal? Easy to secure? and vice versa?

What to steal: #

Model Weights #

I speculate that frontier models are about ~10T params, or ~10TB of data. This fits onto one external hard drive you could buy on Amazon. Cursed.⁠[2] With the weights you instantly have your adversary’s best model. Even if the Anthropic trained a model to do only Anthropic’s bidding, it would be trivial for the CCP to undo this. Models are or will soon be good enough that they can improve themselves, subject to the constraings on how many experiments they can run on your compute. So if China gets GPT 10 the could be off to the races.

Algorithmic Secrets #

Questions:

  • Smart people (Dario, RAND, AIFP) claim that a significant portion of algorithmic secrets can be conveyed in a couple of pages of text.
  • Chinese labs have the human capital to do a frontier training run using these secrets.
  • Frontier runs are aobut 10% of R&D compute. Claude and I speculate that GPT 6 Astra took 9.4e26 flops total,⁠[3] trained on hundreds of thousands of Blackwells in Abilene. China has about 2e21 total flops, so it would take them 5 days to do an Astra-sized training run if they spent all of their compute on it, compared to the 90 days it took OpenAI at Abilene. Realistically, if China used their biggest site in Johor, Malaysia, it would take them about 130 days to train Astra.[^Johor]

[^Johor] China’s biggest site is in Johor because it’s illegal to ship Nvidia chips to China, but it’s not illegal to sell them to a Malaysian company who then rents the compute to China. The Johor site is x H100e, about y% as large as the biggest US site (Stargate in Abilene, Texas). It’s owned by Bain, operated by Oracle, and used by Bytedance, originally for TikTok.

Data, RL Environments #

Algorithmic secrets are enough to make an excellent pretrain, but you can’t posttrain without RL environments. US labs spend tens of billions every year (about $8 billion in 2026) on data and environments.

Notes #

  1. MSS is the CCP’s intelligence service, something like their FBI/CIA/NSA combined. ↩︎

  2. Lots of security proposals involved limiting the bandwidth between some section of the data that holds model weights and the ‘outside world’ (in this case researcher’s laptops). However model weights need to be streaming tokens during inference and saving checkpoints during training. The training issue is easily solved: let your egress limit apply to one permieter which holds both your GPUs and the object storate which holds checkpoints. Inference limits are more challenging: labs serve ~1 trillion tokens from frontier models per day, which is ~3e13 bits of information, or 370 Mbits/s. This means that the fiber which sends out inference tokens can be used to egress weights in 2.5 days. ↩︎

  3. This is Epoch’s estimate (2026): 100,000 GB200s × 90 days (7.8e6 seconds) × 1.2e15 useful FLOP/s per GB200 (25% of its FP8 peak) = 9.3e26, listed as 9.4e26. OpenAI has only said “more than 100,000 GPUs” (2026); the 90 days and the 25% are Epoch’s assumptions. China’s number is IFP’s 4.6M H100-equivalents (2026) at the same utilization. A GB200 is 2.54 H100-equivalents, so 4.6M × 1.2e15 / 2.54 = 2.2e21 useful FLOP/s, and 9.4e26 / 2.2e21 = 4.3e5 seconds = 5 days. ↩︎
