Scattered Thoughts on Datacenter Security

★★★☆☆

Published November 23, 2026Last edited November 23, 2026

Epistemic status: I don’t work at a lab, and I don’t have close friends who do, and I’ve never done large (>x flops) training runs, but I have spent ~200 hours of my life thinking about these topics. So take all numbers and claims in this post as the best guess of an informed amateur armed with lots of AI help.

The AI Safety community is correctly freaking out about security at frontier AI labs. If we can’t make a deal with China to pause, and the US pauses unilaterally, and then China steals model weights, then we are forced to race again to not fall behind.

Here are some questions I hope to deconfuse myself about:

  • The world seems to believe that frontier labs are thoroughly infiltrated, both in cyberspace and by employees who are MSS.⁠[1] Yet the labs seem to care a lot about security.
  • There are many things the CCP could steal: weights, algorithms, RL environments, RL rollouts, source code, etc. What is important? What is likely to go?
  • How to model the CCP? Asleep at the wheel? Very wise to AGI and acting clueless so that they can steal the weights at the right moment?
  • what things are easy to steal? Easy to secure? and vice versa?

What to Steal: #

Model Weights #

I speculate that frontier models are about ~10T params, or ~10TB of data. This fits onto one external hard drive you could buy on Amazon. Cursed.⁠[2] With the weights you instantly have your adversary’s best model. Even if Anthropic trained a model to do only Anthropic’s bidding, it would be trivial for the CCP to undo this. Models are or will soon be good enough that they can improve themselves, subject to the constraints on how many experiments they can run on your compute. So if China gets GPT 10 they could be off to the races.

Algorithmic Secrets #

Questions:

  • Smart people (Dario, RAND, AIFP) claim that a significant portion of algorithmic secrets can be conveyed in a couple of pages of text.
  • Chinese labs have the human capital to do a frontier training run using these secrets.
  • Frontier runs are about 10% of R&D compute. Claude and I speculate that GPT 6 Astra took 9.4×10269.4\times10^{26}9.4×1026 flops total,⁠[3] trained on over 100k Blackwells in Abilene. China has about 2×10212\times10^{21}2×1021 total flops, so it would take them 5 days to do an Astra-sized training run if they spent all of their compute on it, compared to the 90 days it took OpenAI at Abilene. Realistically, if China used their biggest site in Johor, Malaysia, it would take them about 130 days to train Astra.⁠[4]

SFT Data #

I estimate that frontier models SFT on ~1 token per param, based on the fact that Olmo SFTs on 1.4 tokens per param.⁠[5] Thus frontier models use tens of terabytes of SFT data.

RL Environments #

Algorithmic secrets are enough to make an excellent pretrain, but you can’t posttrain without RL environments. US labs spent (about $8 billion in 2026) on data and environments, about 5% of compute spend.

I have very high uncertainty about how many RL environments it takes to train a frontier model. I speculate that it takes millions of environments,⁠[6] which are on average tens of megabytes of data,⁠[7] meaning that stealing every environment is maybe tens of terabytes, the same order of magnitude as the model itself. Possibly you can get away with orders of magnitude fewer bytes via some compression scheme (eg LLM summarization or zstd).

RL Rollouts #

Stealing RL rollouts instead of environments allows you to train a slightly worse model for less compute. If you steal the rollout, you can prefill it and run one backward pass. Prefill is about 5x cheaper than decode based on public API pricing. Your model would be worse if trained this way: RL is much less effective when run off-policy. How much worse is not clear to me.

One rollout on an agentic environment would be about 500,000 tokens, or 2 MB of text. And you might run your model tens or hundreds of times per task. This means that RL rollouts would be 1 or 2 OOMs more data than the environments themselves, maybe hundreds of terabytes.

There are likely many tricks you can do to make this number small, e.g. pulling only successful rollouts and compressing or dropping tool calls.

My takeaways from writing this article:

  • almost anything you could want to steal from a US AI lab would fit on a hard drive inside your clutch.
  • Bandwidth limits are surprisingly effective: even if you directed 100% of Anthropic’s inference bandwidth towards stealing weights, it would still take you a couple days to get them out.

Notes #

  1. MSS is the CCP’s intelligence service, something like their FBI/CIA/NSA combined. ↩︎

  2. Lots of security proposals involved limiting the bandwidth between some section of the data that holds model weights and the ‘outside world’ (in this case researchers’ laptops). However model weights need to be streaming tokens during inference and saving checkpoints during training. The training issue is easily solved: let your egress limit apply to one perimeter which holds both your GPUs and the object storage which holds checkpoints. Inference limits are more challenging: labs serve ~1 trillion tokens from frontier models per day, which is ~3×10133\times10^{13}3×1013 bits of information, or 370 Mbits/s. This means that the fiber which sends out inference tokens can be used to egress weights in 2.5 days. ↩︎

  3. This is Epoch’s estimate (2026): 100,000 GB200s × 90 days (7.8×1067.8\times10^67.8×106 seconds) × 1.2×10151.2\times10^{15}1.2×1015 useful FLOP/s per GB200 (25% of its FP8 peak) = 9.3×10269.3\times10^{26}9.3×1026, listed as 9.4×10269.4\times10^{26}9.4×1026. OpenAI has only said “more than 100,000 GPUs” (2026); the 90 days and the 25% are Epoch’s assumptions. China’s number is IFP’s 4.6M H100-equivalents (2026) at the same utilization. A GB200 is 2.54 H100-equivalents, so 4.6×106×1.2×1015/2.54=2.2×10214.6\times10^6\times1.2\times10^{15}/2.54=2.2\times10^{21}4.6×106×1.2×1015/2.54=2.2×1021 useful FLOP/s, and 9.4×1026/2.2×1021=4.3×1059.4\times10^{26}/2.2\times10^{21}=4.3\times10^59.4×1026/2.2×1021=4.3×105 seconds = 5 days. ↩︎

  4. China’s biggest site is in Johor because it’s illegal to ship Nvidia chips to China, but it’s not illegal to sell them to a Malaysian company who then rents the compute to China. Just three days ago (Nov 19), the Commerce Department started requiring licenses to ship chips to datacenters which in turn serve Chinese companies, and intimated that they would enforce this restriction retroactively, ie require existing datacenter operators to get a license to serve Chinese companies, under threat of future export controls. ‘Recommendation algorithms’ (TikTok algos) are exempt; it’s unclear to what extent this loophole will be abused. ↩︎
