This list is not complete. I plan to update it periodically.

## Authors

1. Scott Alexander ([Astral Codex Ten](https://www.astralcodexten.com/?sort=top),
   [Slate Star Codex](https://slatestarcodex.com/top-posts/))

   1. How I feel reading Alexander is how I imagine a 13-year-old Isaac Newton would feel reading a
      Calc BC textbook. So many patterns that I was barely conscious of he has named, explained, and
      expanded, in ways that consistently change my opinions and make me laugh.

2. Michael Lewis

   1. “I would read an 800-page history of the stapler if he wrote it.”— someone else

3. Nassim Taleb

   1. Taleb chose to put this on the back of _Skin in the Game_ instead of some fluff from an NYT
      reviewer: "The problem isn't that Taleb is an asshole. He is an asshole. The problem is that
      he's right" — attributed to Dan from the Czech Republic on Twitter[^1]

4. Christopher McDougall
5. Michael Pollan
6. Paul Graham

   1. I have read none of his books, but the [essays](https://www.paulgraham.com/) on his website
      are spectacular. I care about startups and programming less than PG does, so I tend to avoid
      essays on these topics.
   2. His best work, in my opinion: [Cities and Ambition](https://www.paulgraham.com/cities.html),
      [Lies We Tell Kids](https://www.paulgraham.com/lies.html),
      [It’s Charisma, Stupid](https://www.paulgraham.com/charisma.html),
      [The Submarine](https://www.paulgraham.com/submarine.html#f4n),
      [The Top of My Todo List](https://www.paulgraham.com/todo.html),
      [How to Disagree](https://www.paulgraham.com/disagree.html)
