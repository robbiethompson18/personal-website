# Research: Theranos Early Red Flags and Investors

Answer to the `@C` note in FINAL_POST.md: "were there signs of Theranos being sus early on? Who were
the investors?" Compiled by Claude, 2026-11-27. Reference point: Carreyrou's WSJ exposé, Oct 15,
2015 (https://www.wsj.com/articles/theranos-has-struggled-with-blood-tests-1444881901).

**Short answer.** Yes, there were many signs, and specialists kept spotting them. A Stanford
pharmacology professor (2002), a med-tech VC (2004), Pfizer (2008), Walgreens' own lab consultant
(2010), the FDA via the Army (2012) and GV's life-science team (2013) all saw through it or walked
away. The money came mostly from rich families with no biotech background: Walton, Murdoch, DeVos,
Cox. Biotech-specialist VCs largely stayed out.

## Early Red Flags (Before Oct 2015)

- **2002, Phyllis Gardner.** The Stanford medicine professor told 19-year-old Holmes her
  antibiotic-patch idea wouldn't work. Holmes came back with the same pitch.
  https://www.businessinsider.in/the-stanford-professor-who-rejected-one-of-elizabeth-holmess-early-ideas-explains-what-it-was-like-to-watch-the-rise-and-fall-of-theranos/articleshow/68468364.cms
- **Jul 2004, MedVenture Associates (a med-tech VC).** Annette Campbell-White said Holmes talked
  "from 30,000 feet" with no technical detail, got annoyed and walked out. Theranos disputes this.
  Sources: the WSJ exposé, and _Bad Blood_ via
  https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/theranos-trial-elizabeth-holmes-investors-venture-capital
- **Nov 2006, Novartis demo.** A reader malfunctioned and a fake result was sent over from
  California. CFO Henry Mosley told Holmes they couldn't keep "fooling investors," and she fired
  him.
  https://files.cand.uscourts.gov/files/18-CR-258%20USA%20v.%20Holmes/Trial%20Exh.%2015017/Trial%20Exh.%2015017%20Email.pdf
  , https://www.cfobrew.com/stories/2025/08/14/preventing-the-theranos-fraud
- **2007, Ana Arriola.** The chief design architect, hired from Apple, quit within months over a
  study testing the unready device on late-stage cancer patients.
  https://goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/theranos-employees-describe-culture-secrecy-elizabeth-holmes-startup-60544673
- **Late 2007, Avie Tevanian.** The ex-Apple exec left the board after questioning the finances,
  then faced legal threats. The only source found is Wikipedia:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theranos
- **2008, Pfizer.** Scientist Shane Weber recommended against a partnership. He called Holmes
  "evasive" and "deflective" and said the conclusions were "not believable." Theranos later showed
  Walgreens a report bearing Pfizer's logo as if it were an endorsement.
  https://www.paloaltoonline.com/news/2021/10/25/prosecutors-elizabeth-holmes-used-fake-pfizer-endorsement-of-theranos-tech-to-get-walgreens-deal
- **2010, Kevin Hunter, Walgreens' lab consultant.**
  - He was never allowed into the lab or shown a live test, and Sunny Balwani escorted him to the
    bathroom.
  - He concluded Theranos was likely "overselling or overstating."
  - A Walgreens executive overruled him: "We can't risk a scenario where CVS has a deal with them in
    six months and it ends up being real."
  - Source:
    https://cases.haas.berkeley.edu/assets/documents/2022-best-case-award-theranos-preview.pdf
- **2011, Johns Hopkins.** Walgreens paid Hopkins to test the device, and Theranos never delivered
  one. https://fortune.com/2016/05/26/walgreens-didnt-verify-theranos/
- **2012, the Army and the FDA.** Lt. Col. David Shoemaker raised Theranos with the FDA, which said
  it wasn't compliant. Holmes asked Gen. Mattis to intervene, and Mattis later joined the board.
  https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/pentagon-officials-emails-demonstrate-that-theranos-concerns-date-back-to/410154/
- **2012–2015, Safeway.** Safeway spent about $350M building clinics in 800 stores. Theranos missed
  every deadline, and the clinics mostly ended up giving flu shots.
  https://fortune.com/2015/11/11/theranos-safeway-deal/
- **2013, GV (Google Ventures).** GV passed. A member of its life-science team took the test at
  Walgreens and was asked for far more than a finger-prick. Caveat: Bill Maris only said this
  publicly after the exposé.
  https://businessinsider.in/bill-maris-heres-why-google-ventures-didnt-invest-in-theranos/amp_articleshow/49475313.cms
- **May 2013, Ian Gibbons.** The chief scientist died by suicide days before he was due to give a
  deposition in a patent suit. His widow says he feared being fired if he told the truth.
  https://www.cbsnews.com/news/theranos-scientist-widow-elizabeth-holmes
- **2013–2014, Adam Rosendorff.** The lab director emailed Holmes "Concerns about the launch" before
  the Walgreens rollout, and quit in Nov 2014.
  https://www.cnbc.com/2021/10/01/elizabeth-holmes-knew-machines-werent-working-says-ex-lab-director.html
- **2014, Tyler Shultz.** He reported manipulated proficiency testing to New York State's Department
  of Health, then quit when his grandfather George Shultz, a board member, sided with Holmes.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Shultz
- **2014–2015, Erika Cheung.** She saw quality-control failures and data points deleted as
  "outliers," and quit in 2014. A Sept 2015 complaint to CMS (Medicare's lab regulator) is later
  attributed to her.
  https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/21/theranos-only-fda-approved-test-is-also-under-scrutiny-after-ex-employee-complaint
- **Dec 2014, Ken Auletta's New Yorker profile.** It called Holmes's explanation of the tech
  "comically vague." Pathologist Adam Clapper read it, blogged his skepticism and tipped off
  Carreyrou. https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2014/12/15/blood-simpler ,
  https://www.darkdaily.com/tag/adam-clapper-md/
- **Feb 2015, John Ioannidis, "Stealth Research," in _JAMA_.** He found no peer-reviewed data; a
  PubMed search turned up only two unrelated papers by Theranos staff.
  https://www.biospace.com/jama-questions-theranos-s-approach-to-science
- **May 2015, Eleftherios Diamandis, in _Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine_.** He concluded
  that most of Theranos's claims were exaggerated.
  https://www.news-medical.net/news/20150630/Biochemistry-professor-examines-the-perceived-success-of-Theranos-test-procedure.aspx
- **The board.** Its members included Shultz, Kissinger, Perry, Nunn, Mattis, Roughead, Kovacevich
  and Bechtel. It wasn't zero medical expertise: Bill Frist (a heart surgeon) and William Foege (a
  former head of the CDC) also sat on it. Fortune's critique of the board ("Plenty of political
  connections, little relevant expertise") ran the same day as the exposé.
  https://fortune.com/2015/10/15/theranos-board-leadership/

## Investors

Theranos raised more than $700M in total (SEC, https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2018-41).
Losses from 2013 onward, from the WSJ's 2018 list via Fortune
(https://fortune.com/2018/05/04/theranos-investment-lost/):

| Investor                    | Amount | Notes                                                           |
| --------------------------- | ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Walton family               | $150M  | 2014                                                            |
| Rupert Murdoch              | $125M  | sold the stake back for $1 in 2017                              |
| DeVos family                | $100M  | planned $50M, doubled it after meeting Holmes (trial testimony) |
| Cox family                  | $100M  |                                                                 |
| Partner Fund Management     | $96.1M | a healthcare hedge fund; sued and settled in 2017               |
| Investors via venture funds | $70M   |                                                                 |
| Carlos Slim                 | ~$30M  |                                                                 |
| Andreas Dracopoulos         | $25M   |                                                                 |
| Oppenheimer family          | $20M   | Fortune omits the unit; probably $20M                           |
| Riley Bechtel               | $6.2M  | also a board member                                             |
| Robert Kraft                | $1M    |                                                                 |

- **How the families came in.** Daniel Mosley, an estate lawyer at Cravath whom Kissinger had asked
  to vet Theranos, introduced the Waltons, Coxes and DeVoses, all his clients. That's $350M from
  three families through one lawyer.
  https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2021/nov/04/holmes-backers-met-via-lawyer/
- **Walgreens, $140M.** It came as a $40M convertible loan plus a $100M "innovation payment."
  Walgreens sued and settled for under $30M. **Safeway** loaned $30M.
- **Early money.**
  - Tim Draper / DFJ led the 2004 seed. Sources disagree on the amount ($500K or $1M). Draper was a
    neighbor and the father of Holmes's childhood friend.
    https://fortune.com/2018/05/11/tim-draper-theranos-elizabeth-holmes/
  - ATA Ventures led the 2006 Series B ($9.1M).
  - Larry Ellison invested through Tako Ventures and Don Lucas was board chairman. Neither amount is
    public.
- **The pattern mostly holds.** Specialists who looked closely passed: MedVenture, Pfizer, GV.
  Walgreens' consultant was overruled. Almost none of the money came from traditional VCs
  (https://www.saastr.com/venture-capitalists-invested-theranos-fall-elizabeth-holmes-scam).
- **The counterexample.** Partner Fund Management was a healthcare-specialist fund. It did real
  diligence and still invested, after being told false things about military use. So "biotech VCs
  stayed out" is accurate; "healthcare experts stayed out" is not.
