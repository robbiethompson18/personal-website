As we march towards the singulariy, I am finally ready to be responsible a do some doomsday
prepping. It would be irresponsible to not have a couple months worth of food and some fuel.

But I am dodging the real question: _do I want to buy a gun_?

Almost certainly I think the answer is yes. It costs only $1k to buy a nice pistol, which I can put
into a safe and never touch. Then if anything does happen, I have not only food, but the means to
keep it. I would of course share, to the extent that doing so is reasonable and humane. But I would
rather make this choice myself. If the government is not around to feed me, it will not be around to
protect me.

Yet something does not sit right with me at all about this decision.

## Morality

1.

I am not a violent person. I have never been in a fistfight. I'm not sure I _could_ shoot another
human being. But I don't think this matters. I can shoot a gun into the air, someone else can hear
it, and they can go away. I can give the gun to a loved one.

2.

Who am I to try to defend any of my property during the apocalypse? Who's to say that I need it more
than anyone else? _I am_, I guess. I defend my property from thieves today, in normal times. The
average human's nature would only be worse during the apocalypse.

3.

Does owning a gun make me paranoid? Probably. But so did buying all that food. It's not clear why
one opens you up to so much more judgment than the other.

The reasoning above makes clear that the thing I want to purchase is a _weapon_. Of self-defense,
but a weapon none the less. A pistol makes the most sense to me; it's portable and easy to use.

A Smith & Wesson M&P9 costs $650. I can buy one at City Arms in Pacifica, 25 minutes from my home. I
need to do a background check first, which takes 10 days.
