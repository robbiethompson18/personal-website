title: Should I Buy a Gun?
date: 2026-11-26
draft: false
---

As we march towards the singularity, I am finally ready to be responsible and do some doomsday
prepping. It would be irresponsible to not have a couple months' worth of food and fuel.

But I am dodging the real question: _do I want to buy a gun_?

Almost certainly the answer is yes. It costs only ~\$1k to buy a nice pistol, which I can put into a
safe and never touch. Then if anything does happen, I have not only food, but the means to keep it.
I would of course share, to the extent that doing so is reasonable and humane. But I would rather
make this choice myself. If the government is not around to feed me, it will not be around to
protect me.

Yet something does not sit right with me at all about this decision.

## Morality

I am not a violent person. I have never been in a fistfight. I'm not sure I _could_ shoot another
human being. But it doesn't matters. I can shoot a gun into the air; the other person will hear it
and go away. I can give the gun to a loved one.

Who am I to try to defend any of my property during the apocalypse? Who's to say that I need it more
than anyone else? _I am_ to say, to decide. I defend my property from thieves today. The average
human's nature would only be worse during the apocalypse.

Does owning a gun make me paranoid? Probably. But so did buying all that food. It's not clear why
one opens me up to so much more judgment than the other.

I refuse to believe that there are simple alternatives. If I have a Smith & Wesson, and my neighbors
have pepper spray, then the crooks are putting on ski goggles and heading to my neighbors'. I could
get a taser, but its range is only 5 yards, they hold charge for 365 days, and each cartridge can
only be used once before needing replacement.

## Logistics

The reasoning above makes clear that the thing I want to purchase is a _weapon_. Of self-defense,
but a weapon nonetheless. A pistol makes the most sense to me; it's portable and easy to use.

A Smith & Wesson M&P9 costs \$650. I can buy one at City Arms in Pacifica, 25 minutes from my home.
I need to do a background check first, which takes ten days. Frustratingly, I have to go once to
take a safety test and pay for the gun, and then return ten days later to retrieve it.

To store the pistol securely, I can buy a safe, which costs \$100, or use the cable lock which comes
with the gun. I will do the former, for the sake of not having a pistol loose in my closet.

I don't have to take any class; all that's required is passing a safety test at the counter before
purchasing.

---

After writing this essay, I'm surprised more people don't buy guns. For less than a thousand
dollars, I will buy the most powerful tool in my home. I hope I never use it.
