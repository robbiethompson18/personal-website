title: Should I Buy a Gun?

---

As we march towards the singulariy, I am finally ready to be responsible a do some doomsday
prepping. It would be irresponsible to not have a couple months worth of food and some fuel.

But I am dodging the real question: _do I want to buy a gun_?

Almost certainly I think the answer is yes. It costs only ~\$1k to buy a nice pistol, which I can
put into a safe and never touch. Then if anything does happen, I have not only food, but the means
to keep it. I would of course share, to the extent that doing so is reasonable and humane. But I
would rather make this choice myself. If the government is not around to feed me, it will not be
around to protect me.

Yet something does not sit right with me at all about this decision.

## Morality

I am not a violent person. I have never been in a fistfight. I'm not sure I _could_ shoot another
human being. But I don't think this matters. I can shoot a gun into the air, someone else can hear
it, and they can go away. I can give the gun to a loved one.

Who am I to try to defend any of my property during the apocalypse? Who's to say that I need it more
than anyone else? _I am_ to say, to decide. I defend my property from thieves today. The average
human's nature would only be worse during the apocalypse.

Does owning a gun make me paranoid? Probably. But so did buying all that food. It's not clear why
one opens you up to so much more judgment than the other.

I refuse to believe that there are simples alternatives. If I have a Smith and Wesson, and my
neighbors have papper spray, then the crooks are putting on ski goggles and heading to my
neighbors'. I could get a taser, but their range is only 5 yards, they hold charge for 365 days, and
they can only be used once before needing replacement.

## Logistics

The reasoning above makes clear that the thing I want to purchase is a _weapon_. Of self-defense,
but a weapon none the less. A pistol makes the most sense to me; it's portable and easy to use.

A Smith & Wesson M&P9 costs \$650. I can buy one at City Arms in Pacifica, 25 minutes from my home.
I need to do a background check first, which takes 10 days. Frustatingly, I have to go once to take
a safety test and pay for the gun, and then return ten days later to retreive it.

To store the pistol securely, I can buy a safe, which costs \$100, or use the cable lock which comes
with the gun. I think I will do the former, for the sake of not having a pistol loose in my closet.

I don't have to take any class, just to take a test at the counter before purchasing.

---

After writing this essay, I'm surprised more people don't buy guns. For less than a thousand
dollars, I will buy the most powerful tool in my home. I hope never to use it.
