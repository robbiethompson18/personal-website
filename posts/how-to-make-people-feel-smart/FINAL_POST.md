---
title: How to Make People Feel Smart
subtitle: And thus ask more questions and learn quickly
date: 2025-01-25
---

I recently quit my job at a quant trading firm. Early on, we were told explicitly: "the number one
mistake new traders make is not asking enough questions." When I heard this, I thought it would be
easy advice to follow.

I was wrong. I fell very far behind in learning new things at work because I didn't ask enough
questions. The funny thing was, I knew I was making this mistake. And I couldn't stop myself.

One reason you might not ask questions is because you don't have any. You're don't
[notice your own confusion](https://www.lesswrong.com/s/zpCiuR4T343j9WkcK). This issue was
insignificant for me. I still had tons of unanswered questions I wasn't asking.

I didn't ask questions at work is because I was embarrassed and self-conscious.

My employer didn't do obviously hurtful or aggressive things that made me feel like my fragile
emotional state was justified. I was almost never yelled at, called stupid, or explicitly compared
to my peers. I was given lots of subtle signals that I was dumb, despite my coworkers best efforts
to not do this.

The advice assumes that people don't ask enough questions, and the marginal one _should_ be
solicited.

## When someone is asking you questions

- Compliment questions.
- Be explicit when you don't know the answer.
- Encourage people to share their learnings if they asked a great question.
- If you're too busy, be apologetic.
- If someone should figure out a question on their own as an exercise, be nice when you communicate
  this.
- Figure out the question behind the question; show interest in someone else's work.
- Always end with "what other questions do you have?"
- When things are hard, say so.

## When You Are Giving a Presentation

- Try not to call things easy, simple, trivial, or straightforward.
- If you feel you've botched an explanation, acknowledge that you've botched it and try again.
- When people ask questions that are out of scope or very advanced, say so to put the rest of the
  room at ease.
