My sister told me last week that my pronunciation changed. I speak clearly and emphasize all my
consonants now. I'm disconcerted; I spent years trying to stop mumbling, and suddenly at 26 I quit
by accident?

## Child Speak

I grew up with four sisters. Our seven person family ate dinner together every night. Everyone is
smart and opinionated. My dad liked to be intellectually provocative. I got most of my words in
while he chewed. I blame my fast mumbling speech on this.[^justso]

[^justso]:
    I worry this is a just-so story and I picked up mumbling from childhood. But I can only think of
    one childhood friend who also mumbles.

## Trader Speak

The most salient part of the Jane Street Dialect is words for designating responsibility. When a
large quote lands, the first thing you'll hear shouted is "MINE" or "YOURS." Almost all statements
get an immediate response like "ack" or "agree" or "weakly disagree." Jane Street spends a lot of
time teaching interns how to use trader lingo properly ("I'm 5 at 8, 7 up"), but this
responsibility-delegation language is more important and needs no purposeful teaching.

An infuriating experience from other workplaces: someone will say or Slack "we should really do x,"
others express agreement, and ... the conversation dies. People think I'm intense when I reply with
"yours Bob" or "mine," but I refuse to unlearn this habit.

## Rat Speak

I pronounce 't's now! Instead of saying "I dunno" or "lil" I say "I don'**t** know" and
"li**tt**le." This is downstream of spending more time with EAs and rationalists. I spent a while
digging into where their consonant overpronunciation originates and could not find a satisfying
answer.

## Math Speak

Both Jane Streeters and Rationalists use math jargon in everyday speech in ways that enrich
conversation:

- The last Lyft bike is _adverse_.
- The match was going poorly, I wanted _vol_.
- Yes I'm attracted to your intellect, it's hard to say what percentage, because my crush on you is
  so _overdetermined_.[^yup]
- Amongst my friends he is on the _Pareto frontier_ of "lives close to me" and "strong" so I asked
  him to help move the piano.
- The invite is a _free option_.

Increasingly I find it frustrating to communicate with people who do not understand statements like
the above.

[^yup]:
    Yes, I really said this to a woman, and she thought this was a perfectly normal thing to say,
    and my crush became that much more overdetermined.

I am guilty of using nerdspeak needlessly, which I find grating when done by others. Examples (with
standard English alternatives in parens).

- The two ideas are _orthogonal_ (unrelated).
- I eat two meals a day _in expectation_ (on average).
- Taking e-bikes _costs epsilon dollars_ (is cheap).
- It is _approximately_ (about) 80° outside.

## Qualifiers

I used to see superfluous qualifiers, like "_I propose_ that pickleball is tennis for narps" or "I
suspect that Chan will lose the election," as nerd-signaling. In reality this is
nerd-signals-deference.[^yud] To signal uncertainty, one says "80% Chan loses the election." To
signal deference, one says "I suspect she will," with the implication "it is my smooth smooth brain
doing the suspecting, you should trust your own intellect over mine."

    ["When (Not) To Use Probabilities"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AJ9dX59QXokZb35fk/when-not-to-use-probabilities)
