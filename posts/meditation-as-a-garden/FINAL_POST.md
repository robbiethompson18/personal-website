---
title: Meditation as a Garden
date: 2025-01-29
updated: 2025-01-30

When I meditate, I like to imagine that my mind is a beautiful garden, and my attention is sitting
inside of it. I aim to observe the garden, to avoid interacting with it.

On some days my garden is full of life. Birds rest in the orchard. Gophers wander around the
vegetable bed, eating the carrots. Fluffy clouds float across the sky.

You might see this activity in your garden as bad or worrisome. _I am not meditating_. _If I was
really meditating, there would be no gophers eating my radishes, no worms eating away at my apples,
no flies buzzing in my ear._ It is not so. Even experienced meditators have hopes, worries, fears,
and dreams. Being worried does not mean you are not meditating. If you notice that you are worried,
you are meditating. Let it be your door into the practice.

Some days, your garden is so overstimulating that you cannot resist moving around in it. Maybe you
went on a date with a beautiful, charming man last night. In your garden he is a hummingbird, his
wings buzzing loudly as he moves from rose to rose, his iridescent feathers catching the sun at
every turn. You cannot resist focusing on him. You get up, you grab your net, and you chase him
around the garden, dodging and weaving and swiping your net through the air.

Your phone buzzes, telling you that your twenty minute meditation is over. You get up, disappointed.
_I did not meditate. If I was meditating I would have noticed the hummingbird, without chasing him_.
But you did this! Your timer rang, you noticed that you were chasing the hummingbird, and you put
down your net, if just for a moment. Do this just once, and you have meditated.

The goal of meditation is not to have a quiet garden; it is to be able to sit still in a loud one.
The act of meditation is not sitting still in the garden. It is trying to sit still in the garden.
