## Headline Findings

- **Q1, every major city river swimmable: yes for the rich world, for roughly $100-150 billion more
  in the US (my estimate, arithmetic below), if "swimmable" means the Copenhagen/Paris standard:
  open on dry days, flagged closed for 1-3 days after heavy rain.** Swimmable every day including
  storm days costs several times more. New York's own figure for citywide swimmable waters was
  [up to $84 billion](https://citylimits.org/the-cost-of-our-waternew-york-confronts-polluted-past-in-its-waterways/)
  (2015), and San Francisco prices full wet-weather capture for its bay side alone at
  [$10.68 billion](https://cdn.kqed.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2024/05/SF-Sewer-System-Fact-Sheet.pdf).
- **Q2, a swimmable natural-rock waterfall in Golden Gate Park: physically easy, legally hard, and
  cold.** The park already has a 110-foot artificial waterfall. Pumping a stream-sized flow up 20 m
  costs about $140,000 a year in electricity. The binding constraints are California pool law (a
  continuous chlorine residual, which a mossy waterfall cannot coexist with), water near 13-17 C
  without heating, and the fact that a waterfall is a cooling tower, so heating it costs several
  times what pumping does.
- The mechanism behind most unswimmable rivers is the combined sewer. About
  [700 US communities](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/where-combined-sewer-overflows-are-located) have
  one. New York still releases about
  [18 billion gallons a year from 398 outfalls](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/water/water-quality/combined-sewer-overflow)
  after
  [$45 billion](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dep/downloads/pdf/water/nyc-waterways/citywide-east-river-open-water/citywide-open-waters-ltcp-executive-summary.pdf)
  of investment.
- Paris spent
  [€1.4 billion](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/30/g-s1-14315/triathlon-seine-water-pollution-delay-paris-olympics),
  and in the first public season (2025) its three Seine sites were open for swimming on
  [114 of 195 scheduled site-days, 58.5%](https://patrickbayeux.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2512-Dds-Apur-bilan-baignade-Seine.pdf).
  They still drew 100,000 swimmers, and
  [120,000 in 2026](https://www.linfodurable.fr/saison-estivale-record-pour-les-baignades-paris-58745).
- London's £5 billion tunnel cut overflows
  [86%](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/news/2026/oct/86-drop-in-sewage-discharges-into-thames-as-the-operation-of-thames-tideway-tunnel-transfers-to-thames-water),
  and central London is still closed to swimmers because the port authority bans it for
  [currents and boat traffic](https://haringeycommunitypress.co.uk/2025/01/10/port-of-london-authority-raises-safety-concerns-over-khan-swimmable-rivers-pledge/).
  Clean water is necessary and not sufficient.
- EPA's tally of remaining US combined-sewer work fell from
  [$84.5 billion (2008) to $36.5 billion (2022)](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-05/2022-cwns-report-to-congress.pdf)
  in constant dollars. More than half the job is already done.
- The cheapest 80% version is not a clean river. It is daily bacteria testing, a flag, a ladder and
  a lifeguard at one site, legalized. Copenhagen's first harbor bath cost
  [DKK 4 million](https://theindexproject.org/post/copenhagen-habour-bath-islands-brygge).
- Unchlorinated public "natural pools" exist (many in Germany and Austria, two in North America),
  but their biofilters cap use at a few hundred to about 1,000 swimmers a day. Minnesota had to
  write [a one-city exemption into statute](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/144.1222) to
  open the first.
- San Francisco ran a version of this experiment:
  [Fleishhacker Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleishhacker_Pool), a 1,000-foot heated seawater
  pool by the zoo, opened 1925, closed 1971. The man who paid for it also paid for Rainbow Falls.

## Part 1: Swimmable City Rivers

### Why Most Urban Rivers Fail

**Combined sewer overflow (CSO).** Cities sewered before about 1900 built one pipe network for both
toilets and street drains. In dry weather everything goes to the treatment plant. The interceptor to
the plant is sized for a small multiple of dry-weather flow and a rainstorm delivers far more, so
the system has relief weirs: when the pipe fills, the mix of rain and raw sewage spills over the
weir into the river. The weirs are deliberate; without them sewage backs up into basements. EPA
counts about [700 communities](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/where-combined-sewer-overflows-are-located)
with combined systems, mostly in the Northeast and Great Lakes
([GAO, 2023](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105285), agrees; EPA's 2004 count was 746
communities and 9,348 outfalls). Its last national volume estimate was about
[850 billion gallons a year](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/csosso_rtc_factsheet.pdf)
(2004).

**The standard.** "Swimmable" is a bacteria count. Regulators measure fecal indicator bacteria, not
the pathogens, and set the threshold by accepted illness rate: EPA's 2012 criteria accept about
[36 gastrointestinal illnesses per 1,000 swimmers](https://colorado-stormwater-council.org/sites/default/files/document-files/2020-01/Wirick%20National%20Progress%20and%20Lessons%20Learned_0.pdf).

| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| [US EPA 2012](https://epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-10/documents/rec-factsheet-2012.pdf), fresh water | 126 (410)          | 35 (130)               | 30-day geometric mean (90th-percentile value) |
| [EU Directive 2006/7/EC](https://legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/7/annexes/adopted), inland "excellent"     | 500                | 200                    | 95th percentile                               |

EPA constrains the average; the EU constrains the tail. Raw sewage carries millions of E. coli per
100 mL (unverified), so a small spill moves a river from pass to fail by orders of magnitude, and it
takes one to three days of flow and sunlight to recover. That decay time is why every swimmable city
runs a flag system.

**Secondary causes.** (a) Legacy sediment: PCBs, dioxin, mercury, coal tar in the river bed,
independent of sewers. (b) Runoff in cities with separate sewers: dog waste, leaking laterals and
illicit connections reach the river untreated. (c) Upstream farms and towns: the Seine's July 2025
closures were blamed on
[rain raising pollution upstream](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/with-the-seine-open-for-swimming-in-paris-tourists-and-residents-embrace-it-as-temperatures-soar).
(d) Currents and boats, the reason central London stays closed even when clean.

**Paris.** The Olympic cleanup built the Austerlitz basin to hold storm flow until the plants can
take it. The men's triathlon was
[postponed a day on 30 July 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/07/30/g-s1-14315/triathlon-seine-water-pollution-delay-paris-olympics)
after rain, and the Paralympic one slipped too. Public sites opened 5 July 2025 at Bras Marie, Bercy
and Grenelle and closed the next day for rain. The planning agency APUR's report (2025) has the
cleanest numbers anyone has published:
[Bercy open for swimming 37 of 72 scheduled days (51%), Bras Marie 40 of 58 (69%), Grenelle 37 of 65 (57%); 100,000 swimmers](https://patrickbayeux.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2512-Dds-Apur-bilan-baignade-Seine.pdf),
877 per site per open day, with one site near 2,500 a day in the 35 C heat of 12 August. In 2026 the
three sites took
[120,000 swimmers in eight weeks, up 20%](https://www.linfodurable.fr/saison-estivale-record-pour-les-baignades-paris-58745),
with brief closures for a fuel-oil spill. One conflict: the prefecture said in 2025 that
[Bras Marie would not reopen](https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/news/in-paris/articles/332241-paris-why-the-bras-marie-bathing-site-will-not-reopen-in-2026)
because of boat traffic, yet 2026 reports list it as open. My arithmetic: €1.4 billion over about
220,000 Seine swims so far is €6,400 per swim, a figure that falls every year and ignores the river
itself
([31 fish species in 2024 against 10 in 1990](https://patrickbayeux.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2512-Dds-Apur-bilan-baignade-Seine.pdf)).

**London.** The tunnel was fully connected in February 2025. Is the tidal Thames swimmable? A
[2012 Port of London Authority byelaw](https://server1.pla.co.uk/Safety/Swimming-in-the-tidal-Thames)
requires written permission to swim between Putney Bridge and Crossness; from Putney up to
Teddington it is permitted and discouraged. The PLA's answer to the mayor's swimmable-rivers pledge
was that the tidal Thames
["will never be safe to swim in"](https://haringeycommunitypress.co.uk/2025/01/10/port-of-london-authority-raises-safety-concerns-over-khan-swimmable-rivers-pledge/).
London's first designated Thames bathing water,
[at Ham and Kingston, dates from 15 May 2026](https://www.richmond.gov.uk/bathing_site); it had a
blue-green algae warning by August.

**Boston.** In October 1995 EPA set the goal: the lower Charles
[swimmable and fishable by Earth Day 2005](https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/charlesriver/epa-sets-clean-goal-charles-river-earth-day-2005_.html).
The first report card was a D. By 1998 it was a C+ with swimming standards met
[51% of the time](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/b3380e8da0da4642852574b0005daf83.html).
Twenty-one years past the deadline there is one public swim day, City Splash:
[canceled in 2025 for bacteria after rain](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/boston/news/charles-river-swim-canceled-city-splash),
back in
[July 2026 with 300+ swimmers](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/07/18/metro/charles-river-swimming-city-splash/).
The
[2025 grades](https://www.crwa.org/river-current/2025-charles-river-report-card-understanding-the-grades)
run A to C by segment.

**Chicago.** The first sanctioned river swim since the 1920s ran on
[21 September 2025 with about 300 swimmers](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2025/09/21/hundreds-plunge-into-chicago-river-in-citys-first-open-water-swim-in-nearly-a-century).
The 2026 edition was reportedly
[canceled at 4 a.m. for rain](https://www.karmactive.com/chicago-river-swim/) (single source).
Tunnels reduce overflows; they do not end them.

**Not there yet.** Berlin has banned Spree swimming since 1925. The Flussbad group wants an
[840 m stretch of the Spree canal](https://www.thelocal.de/20250620/will-berlin-open-up-the-spree-river-to-swimmers);
a 2026 pilot site did not happen, and an
[August 2026 Senate answer](https://pardok.parlament-berlin.de/starweb/adis/citat/VT/19/SchrAnfr/S19-26759.pdf)
calls the canal unsuitable. New York's +POOL, a floating pool that filters East River water through
its walls, has a finished barge and a
[2027 install date](https://pluspool.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/260729_For-Immediate-Release_Resilience-Work-Continues-through-Fall-.pdf),
sixteen years after it was proposed. Seoul allows the Han only for event swims and runs filtered
riverside pools that drew
[nearly 600,000 in summer 2026](https://koreatimes.co.kr/lifestyle/people-events/20260907/nighttime-splashes-draw-nearly-600000-to-seouls-han-river-parks-this-summer).
Vienna swims in the Old and New Danube side channels,
[all rated good or excellent](https://wien.gv.at/umwelt/badewasserqualitaet), not the main stem.
Rotterdam has one legal city-centre site,
[Rijnhaven](https://www.uitagendarotterdam.nl/en/all-articles/in-on-on-and-under-water-in-rotterdam/).

### The Count

Nobody publishes a ranking. The [Swimmable Cities alliance](https://www.swimmablecities.org),
launched in July 2024 with a ten-point charter whose first principle is the "right to swim", lists
about 255 signatory organisations in 121 cities and towns in 38 countries. Most are NGOs plus a few
city halls (the mayors of Paris, London and Portland
[have signed](https://www.outdoorswimmingsociety.com/swimmable-cities/)), so it is a list of
intentions. My count, as judgment: of the roughly 40 largest rich-world cities on a river or harbor,
about 10 offer legal everyday swimming in the main water body (Copenhagen, Zurich, Basel, Bern,
Munich, Oslo, Stockholm, Helsinki, Sydney, Portland), about 8 more allow it at flagged sites or on
event days (Paris, outer London, Rotterdam, Vienna, Boston, Chicago, Washington, Seoul), and the
rest ban it. Outside the rich world the count by any bathing standard is near zero, though far more
people are in the water.

Is the Anacostia swimmable now? No. It is
[still illegal to swim](https://thedailyrecord.com/2024/06/05/forgotten-river-running-from-md-through-dc-cleans-up-slowly/)
without a special permit, and the one permitted event, planned since 2023, has been
[canceled repeatedly for bacteria](https://ggwash.org/view/amp/97056).

**My estimate, US, "open on dry days" standard:**

1. Remaining CSO work: $36.5 billion reported, times 1.5 to 2 for EPA's stated undercount = **$55-75
   billion**. Sanity check: the same line was $84.5 billion in 2008, so about $48 billion was worked
   off in 14 years, $3.4 billion a year.
2. Runoff in separate-sewer cities (Los Angeles, Houston, Atlanta): a third to a half of the $115.3
   billion stormwater need = **$40-58 billion**. The share is my assumption.
3. Toxic sediment: about a dozen urban reaches at roughly $1 billion each = **$10-15 billion**.
4. Sum: $105-148 billion, **call it $100-150 billion**. Over 20 years that is $5-7.5 billion a year,
   or $15-22 per American per year (335 million people).

Bottom-up cross-check: big-city programs run $1.4-4.7 billion, median about $3 billion. If about 40
of the 700 combined-sewer communities are large cities (my count, unverified), that is $120 billion
of total program, perhaps half spent, which lands on line 1.

**What this does not buy** is a swim the day after a storm. Permit compliance still means a handful
of overflows a year (Portland about 4, San Francisco about 10). Eliminating them is where cost goes
vertical: SFPUC says capturing an average year's overflow from its central bay side needs a
[24-foot-diameter tunnel 13 miles long](https://cdn.kqed.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2024/05/SF-Sewer-System-Fact-Sheet.pdf)
in a city 7 miles wide, and it would still spill in the biggest storms.

**Global, much rougher.** In 2020,
[44% of the world's household wastewater was discharged without safe treatment](https://www.unwater.org/app/uploads/2021/09/SDG6_Indicator_Report_631_Progress-on-Wastewater-Treatment_2021_EN.pdf)
(WHO and UN-Habitat, 2021). If about 2 billion city dwellers need sewers and treatment at $500-1,500
a head (both numbers are my assumptions), the bill is **$1-3 trillion**. The rich-world last mile is
small by comparison: Paris, London, Chicago, Portland and Washington together spent about $16
billion.

### Hard Cases Where Sewers Are Not the Constraint

**Superfund rivers.** The contaminant is in the mud, so a perfect sewer still leaves a river you
should not wade in. Gowanus Canal: the 2013 plan was $506 million; the sewage retention tanks alone
are now
[over $1 billion, a 1,300% increase on the original estimate](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/EPA/epaoig20240321-24-p-0029cert.pdf)
(EPA Inspector General, 2024). Lower Passaic:
[$1.38 billion to dredge 3.5 million cubic yards](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epa/newsreleases/epa-finalizes-passaic-river-cleanup-one-largest-superfund-projects-epa-history-will.html)
of dioxin-laden sediment, decided in 2016 and
[still fought over in 2025](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/10082025/new-jersey-contaminated-passaic-river-superfund-site/).
Lower Duwamish: $342 million in the 2014 plan,
[$668 million in the March 2026 settlement](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-reaches-668m-settlement-agreement-continued-cleanup-lower-duwamish-waterway).
Newtown Creek has no final remedy yet. These run for decades from listing to finish.

**Ganges and Yamuna.** Namami Gange has sanctioned
[492 projects worth ₹40,120 crore (about $4.7 billion) and spent ₹18,842 crore of the ₹30,096 crore allocated by March 2025, 63%](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/namami-gange-programme-assessing-progress-as-phase-ii-nears-its-2026-deadline/).
Sewage treatment capacity rose about thirty-fold to 3,446 million litres a day. Dissolved oxygen and
pH now meet bathing norms at every monitored point and fecal coliform (Indian bathing limit: 2,500
per 100 mL) has fallen at several ghats, with Uttar Pradesh partly improved and Bihar barely. Money
is not the constraint. Sewer connections lag treatment plants, and dry-season flow is diverted for
irrigation, so there is little dilution. I found no comparable figures for the Yamuna in Delhi.

**Citarum.** Indonesia's 2018 Citarum Harum program promised
[drinkable water by 2025](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2018/03/24/drinkable-water-mission-impossible.html)
against an estimated need of
[34.8 trillion rupiah (~$2.3 billion)](https://www.eco-business.com/news/cleanup-of-worlds-dirtiest-river-banks-on-mobilised-java-communities).
The river improved and is still classed as polluted; in 2025 the program's budget was
[at risk of being cut](https://megapolitan.antaranews.com/berita/354169/jawa-barat-susun-strategi-untuk-citarum-harum).
**Pasig.** Manila's river, once declared biologically dead, is getting
[an esplanade](https://mb.com.ph/2025/10/18/the-pasig-river-revival-milestone-for-sustainability-and-heritage)
and trash interception. I found no bacteria data suggesting swimming is near.

### San Francisco

San Francisco is
[the only coastal California city with a combined system](https://cdn.kqed.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2024/05/SF-Sewer-System-Fact-Sheet.pdf),
and an unusual one: the city ringed itself with large underground transport-and-storage boxes that
hold storm flow for the plants. Since 1976 it has spent over $2 billion and cut discharge volume 80%
from 7.6 billion gallons a year; of about 30 storms a year, about 10 now cause a discharge, and what
spills is about [94% stormwater](https://www.sfpuc.gov/programs/ocean-and-beach-monitoring),
partially treated. The US and California
[sued the city in May 2024](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/press_room/press_releases/2024/pr20240501-epa-san-francisco.html)
over those discharges anyway, alleging an average of 1.8 billion gallons a year since 2016. The
city's counter-offer is $2.36 billion of projects over 15 years.

**Where you can swim.** [Aquatic Park](https://www.swimmersguide.com/locations/2463) (a sheltered
cove, 10-16 C year-round), Crissy Field, China Beach, Baker Beach. Ocean Beach is legal and has
lethal rip currents. SFPUC and the health department
[sample weekly and post a beach](https://www.sfpuc.gov/programs/ocean-and-beach-monitoring) after
any combined sewer discharge that reaches it. Heal the Bay's pattern for the city is A and B grades
in dry summer weather and poor wet-weather grades; Candlestick Point has made the statewide
worst-ten list (unverified for the current edition).

**Where you cannot.** Mission Creek and Islais Creek are the old stream channels that receive the
central bay side's overflows; the regional board ordered the city in 2023 to
[characterize their sediment contamination](https://cdn.kqed.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2024/05/SF-Sewer-System-Fact-Sheet.pdf).
Lake Merced, the Golden Gate Park lakes and Oakland's Lake Merritt are closed to swimming. Oakland
Public Works told KQED there are
[no plans for a swimming beach](https://www.kqed.org/news/10900605/what-would-it-take-to-make-lake-merritt-swimmable)
at Lake Merritt: most of the water probably would not make you sick, but the city will not accept
the risk from sewer leaks.

**Harbor-bath proposals.** One: a floating 50 m pool at Piers 30-32, proposed by Strada and Trammell
Crow in 2022, with a state enabling bill (SB 273) passed in 2023. In July 2025 the Port and the
developer
[paused negotiations for 18 months](https://www.sfport.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/item_11b_piers_30-32_feasibility_improvement_ideas_-_info.docx.pdf)
over feasibility and cost. I found no Oakland proposal.

### Benefits

- **Use.** 100,000 then 120,000 Seine swims a season at three small sites; 600,000 at Seoul's
  riverside pools.
- **Illness.** Surface-water recreation in the US causes an estimated
  [90 million illnesses a year costing $2.2-3.7 billion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5759255)
  (DeFlorio-Barker et al., 2018). Cleaner water cuts that; more swimmers add to it.
- **Property.** A hedonic study found sediment cleanup raised nearby home prices in
  [Milwaukee](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/F3891A8BE6A3544390E0DA3BADEEDFF8/core-reader)
  (2022).
- **Heat, the non-obvious one.** In Paris in 2026,
  [a third of the days the sites were open coincided with a heat wave](https://www.linfodurable.fr/saison-estivale-record-pour-les-baignades-paris-58745),
  and the city opened the Canal Saint-Martin early during one in June. In APUR's 2025 survey,
  cooling off was the second most cited reason to come (27%, after the setting at 31%). Old European
  housing has no air conditioning, so a free river is public cooling with no electricity bill. The
  cost is drowning: Switzerland averages about
  [47 drownings a year](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/switzerland/58-people-drowned-in-switzerland-in-li.2414491),
  most in open water.

### Verdict (My Judgment)

"Every major city" is achievable in rich countries within 20-30 years at the dry-day standard, for
about $100-150 billion in the US, most of it already mandated by consent decrees. It is not
achievable at a sane price at the every-day standard, and in several cities currents and shipping
block it regardless. For Ganges-class rivers it is a multi-generation sanitation project. The
cheapest 80%: pick one calm site per city, test daily with rapid methods, fly a flag, add steps, a
rope line and a lifeguard, and legalize it. Sydney's netted enclosures and Copenhagen's DKK 4
million bath show the hardware is nearly free once the water passes on most dry days. Where it does
not, a filtering barge like +POOL gives you the river without waiting for the river.

## Part 2: A Swimmable Natural-Rock Waterfall in Golden Gate Park

### It Partly Exists

**Huntington Falls** on Strawberry Hill is
[110 feet tall](https://richmondsunsetnews.com/2024/03/01/looking-back-huntington-falls/). Water
first ran on 8 April 1894, paid for by a $25,000 gift from the railroad magnate Collis Huntington.
It is a closed loop: a reservoir on the hilltop feeds the falls, which drop into the artificial lake
around the hill (Stow Lake, renamed Blue Heron Lake in 2024), and lake water is pumped back up. On
24 July 1962 a pipe broke, washed out the earth behind the cement-and-sandstone face, and the falls
collapsed. They sat dry for 22 years until an $846,000 restoration; Mayor Feinstein rededicated them
on 12 June 1984. The lead's facts all check out. **Rainbow Falls** (1930) was
[a gift from the banker and park commissioner Herbert Fleishhacker](https://richmondsunsetnews.com/2023/08/04/looking-back-rainbow-falls/),
who wanted one like the cascade in the Bois de Boulogne; it sits in a former rock quarry below
Prayerbook Cross. Nobody is allowed in either. I found no flow rates or pump ratings for them.

The whole park is engineered. Work began in 1870-71 under the engineer William Hammond Hall on a
site that
["mostly consisted of sand dunes and was believed to be unfit to grow anything"](https://www.theclio.com/entry/38605);
about 60,000 trees went in within four years, and John McLaren ran the park for decades after.
Irrigation came from the park's own aquifer: the
[Dutch Windmill (1902-03) pumped about 30,000 gallons an hour](https://www.illinoiswindmills.org/dutch-windmill)
and the [Murphy Windmill about 40,000](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/murphy-windmill), until
electric pumps replaced them around 1913. The park still runs on its wells, about
[427 million gallons a year](https://www.watereducation.org/aquafornia-news/golden-gate-park-uses-427-million-gallons-drinkable-water-year-thats-all-about),
and is switching to reverse-osmosis recycled water from the Oceanside plant, averaging
[1.6 million gallons a day](https://sfpuc.org/sites/default/files/documents/Westside%20Enhanced%20Recycled%20Water_June_2022.pdf),
with deliveries
[due to start at the end of 2026](https://www.sfpuc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Westside_Enhanced_Water_Recycling_Project_FAQ_August_2024.pdf).

### Precedents for Swimming

**Natural flow-through.**
[Barton Springs](https://www.austintexas.gov/department/barton-springs-pool) in Austin is three
acres, 20-21 C year-round, unchlorinated, and stays clean because
[26-31 million gallons a day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Springs_Pool) of aquifer water
pass through it. [Sliding Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliding_Rock) in North Carolina sends
11,000 gallons a minute of 10-15 C water down a 60-foot rock slide into an 8-foot pool. The Aare
runs about 18-24 C in summer, with a record
[24.8 C in July 2026](https://www.plattformj.ch/artikel/244872/). All three work by dilution: the
water is replaced faster than swimmers can foul it.

**Artificial "natural pools".** Water circulates from a swimming basin through a planted gravel bed
where biofilm and zooplankton strip nutrients and bacteria. There is no disinfectant in the water,
so there is no fast response to contamination; the only controls are a low bather load and closing.

### Design

**Spec.** 20 m drop, 30-40 m wide face, 1,000 m2 plunge pool averaging 1.5 m deep (1,500 m3). The
park has real rock to work with: Rainbow Falls sits in an old quarry face.

**Pumping.** P = ρ g Q h / η, with ρ = 1,000 kg/m3, g = 9.81 m/s2, h = 20 m, η = 0.75 for pump and
motor. Pipe friction adds 10-20%, ignored here.

| Flow Q   | Looks like                          | Power  | kWh/yr at 12 h/day | Annual bill |
| -------- | ----------------------------------- | ------ | ------------------ | ----------- |
| 0.1 m3/s | A few thin veils                    | 26 kW  | 115,000            | ~$28,000    |
| 0.5 m3/s | A full stream; near the Rain Vortex | 131 kW | 573,000            | ~$140,000   |
| 2 m3/s   | A small river                       | 523 kW | 2,290,000          | ~$560,000   |

Middle row worked: 1,000 × 9.81 × 0.5 × 20 / 0.75 = 130,800 W. Times 4,380 hours = 573,000 kWh. Rec
and Park is on Hetch Hetchy Power's municipal "General Use" tariff,
[$0.2008/kWh in summer and $0.1606 in winter plus $23.05 per kW of monthly peak demand](https://www.sfpuc.gov/sites/default/files/accounts-and-services/Rates_Schedule_HHP_CleanPowerSF_2026-7.pdf)
(medium commercial, FY 2026-27). At a blended $0.18: 573,000 × 0.18 = $103,000, plus demand 131 ×
23.05 × 12 = $36,000. The formula checks against Guiyang: 740 kW × 0.75 / (1,000 × 9.81 × 108 m) =
0.52 m3/s. Pumping is not the problem.

**Water.** A recirculating system loses water to evaporation, wind drift, filter backwash and the
fresh water added per swimmer. My estimate is on the order of 100 m3 (26,000 gallons) a day, under
2% of the park's new recycled supply. California allows disinfected tertiary recycled water in
["nonrestricted recreational impoundments"](https://epa.gov/waterreuse/summary-californias-water-reuse-guideline-or-regulation-impoundments),
meaning body contact; whether a health officer would accept it in a permitted public pool is
untested (unverified).

**Temperature.** Shallow groundwater sits near mean annual air temperature, about 14 C in San
Francisco (rule of thumb; I found no measured well temperature). July in the city averages highs
near
[21 C and lows near 13 C](https://weatherspark.com/m/557/7/Average-Weather-in-July-in-San-Francisco-California-United-States),
colder at the park's foggy west end, and the ocean is
[about 14.7 C](https://seatemperature.info/july/ocean-beach-san-francisco-water-temperature.html). A
waterfall is an evaporative cooler: it shatters water into films and droplets with huge surface area
and drags air through them, pulling the water toward the air's wet-bulb temperature. My estimate for
an unheated recirculating falls here is 13-17 C in summer, the same as Aquatic Park. Barton Springs
is 20-21 C; Basel
[advises against entering the Rhine below 18 C](https://www.basel.com/en/activities-excursions/swimming-rhine).
Sliding Rock is 10-15 C and popular, so cold does not kill demand for a thrill. It kills lingering,
and small children lose heat fast.

Heating fights the waterfall directly. If each pass over the falls costs the water 1 C, holding
temperature at Q = 0.5 m3/s takes 0.5 × 4.19 MJ/(m3·K) × 1 K = 2.1 MW of heat. With a heat pump at a
coefficient of performance of 4 that is about 520 kW of electricity, four times the pumping power,
roughly $400,000-550,000 a year. The 1 C per pass is my assumption and could be half or double
depending on wind and humidity. The escape is to split the loops: a cold showpiece falls on one
circuit, a warm lagoon beside it on another. Then you swim next to a waterfall, not under it.
Fleishhacker Pool is the local precedent for betting on heated outdoor water: 1,000 by 150 feet,
lifeguards in boats, closed in 1971 after low use and repair bills the city would not pay.

**Structure and seismic.** A 20 m face is a six-storey retaining structure on dune sand a few miles
from the San Andreas fault. The engineering is routine: a reinforced-concrete core on piles or a
mat, with rock as cladding or boulders pinned to it. The failure mode to design against is the one
that destroyed Huntington Falls in 1962, internal erosion from a leaking pipe. Every pipe goes in an
inspectable gallery.

**Capital cost (my judgment, by analogy).** Rock skin: 2,000-3,000 m2 of face and pool edge at
$75-125 per square foot is $1.6-4 million in artificial rock, more in natural boulders. Reference
points: the 1984 Huntington repair at $846,000 is about $2.7 million today; Webber's whole pool was
$6-7 million; Borden's C$14.4 million. A 20 m structure, a 1,500 m3 pool, a treatment plant sized
for a 0.5 m3/s loop, a bathhouse, accessible routes and San Francisco soft costs put this at
**$40-80 million**, plus $1-2 million a year to operate, mostly lifeguards.

### The Real Blockers

**Pool law.** California defines a public pool broadly, and Title 22 requires it to be
[disinfected continuously with at least 1.0 ppm free chlorine](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/22-CCR-65529)
(3.0 for wading pools and spray grounds). The building code requires the whole volume through the
filters [every 6 hours](https://emd.saccounty.gov/EH/Documents/GENERAL%20INFORMATION%20SHEET.pdf)
(county summary). A 0.5 m3/s waterfall loop turns a 1,500 m3 pool over in 50 minutes, so turnover is
free. The residual is the problem. Chlorine at 1 ppm kills the moss, ferns and algae film that make
an Indonesian waterfall look like one, the cascade strips chlorine out as fast as you dose it, and
the result is bleached rock that smells like a hotel pool. I found no California exemption for
biologically filtered public pools. Minnesota solved this with one sentence:
["A naturally treated swimming pool located in the city of Minneapolis is not a public pool"](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/144.1222)
for purposes of the pool code, subject only to drain-safety rules. On lifeguards, state law requires
them only where a pool is
[wholly artificial and charges a direct fee](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_116045);
otherwise a sign saying there is none suffices.

**Liability.** The helpful-sounding immunities do not apply. Government Code
[831.2](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2005/gov/830-831.9.html) covers injuries from "a
natural condition of any unimproved public property", and a built waterfall is an improvement by
definition. 831.21 extends that to public beaches even with lifeguards; a plunge pool is not a
beach. [831.7](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-831-7/) is the useful one: no
liability to anyone doing a "hazardous recreational activity", which expressly includes "any form of
diving into water from other than a diving board or diving platform" and water contact where no
lifeguard is provided and warning is given. Its exceptions are where a waterfall lives: failure to
warn of a known dangerous condition, negligent construction or maintenance of the structure, and any
case where a specific fee was charged. The cautionary case is the Fort Worth Water Gardens, a Philip
Johnson cascade people walk down into.
[Four people drowned in its pool on 16 June 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth_Water_Gardens);
the city settled for $750,000 and reopened in 2007 with the pool cut from 9 feet deep to 2.
Liability pushes public water features toward water too shallow to swim in.

**ADA.** A pool with under 300 linear feet of wall needs
[one accessible entry, a lift or a sloped entry](https://www.ada.gov/resources/accessible-pools-requirements);
larger pools need two. A beach-style sloped entry satisfies it. The accessible route across rockwork
to the falls is the harder part.

**Historic status and CEQA.** Golden Gate Park has been a
[National Register historic district since 2004](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/NRIS/04001137),
with 133 contributing features. A new six-storey structure there is a change to a historic resource
under CEQA, which means a full environmental impact report and probably litigation. I could not
retrieve the 1998 Park Master Plan to check its policy on new structures.

### Verdict (My Judgment)

Buildable: yes. $40-80 million and $1-2 million a year gets a chlorinated, lifeguarded rock lagoon
with a 20 m falls, permitted as a public pool, which will look and smell like a resort pool and sit
near 15 C unless you spend as much again on heating. The Indonesian version, unchlorinated and
mossy, needs state legislation of the Minnesota kind, and its biofilter then caps use at a few
hundred children a day. Three things nearer to approvable, cheapest first:

1. **Let people into what exists.** Rebuild the base of Huntington Falls or Rainbow Falls as a
   zero-depth walk-under cascade on its own chlorinated loop, regulated as a spray ground: no
   standing water, little drowning exposure. Low single-digit millions (my estimate). Kids get the
   waterfall on their heads, which is most of the point, and rehabilitating an existing feature is
   an easier historic-review argument than a new structure.
2. **A warm lagoon beside a cold falls** on separate loops, on a site outside the park's historic
   core.
3. **A one-city statutory exemption** for a small naturally treated pool with timed tickets, as a
   pilot.

Barton Springs works because 100,000 m3 of clean 20 C water arrives free every day. San Francisco
would be manufacturing the flow, the cleanliness and the warmth, and the third is the expensive one.
