## Headline Findings

- Asteroid deflection is the one planet-scale intervention that has been run as an experiment and
  worked. DART shortened Dimorphos's orbit by 33.0 ± 1.0 minutes, against about 7 minutes expected
  from the spacecraft's momentum alone
  ([Thomas et al., Nature, 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10115635/); NASA's
  first announcement said 32). ESA's Hera reached the asteroid this month: first images came out 12
  November 2026, detailed ones are due 25 November
  ([DLR, 2026](https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2026/hera-spots-its-target-a-first-glimpse-into-the-study-of-the-didymos-binary-asteroid-system)).
- The largest dam removal in history cost about
  [$450 million](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/klamath-dam-removal-project-oregon-california/726000/)
  to
  [$500 million](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/workers-breach-key-klamath-dams-allowing-salmon-swim-freely-first-time-century/CD5ZOU5VUJDL5DF45544QLONU4/?outputType=amp).
  On the Klamath, 7,742 Chinook swam past the old dam sites in 2024 and 13,310 in 2025
  ([USGS, 2026](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/rewilding-upper-klamath-river-basin-rapid-recolonization-chinook-salmon-following)),
  and salmon now occupy
  [88% of their historical range](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/15/salmon-upper-klamath-river-repopulation/)
  in the basin.
- Saving the Great Salt Lake is the cheapest big rescue on the list. It needs about
  [800,000 extra acre-feet a year](https://science.utah.edu/faculty/faculty-research/strike-team-update/),
  and Utah's own 2026 study prices the cheapest farm water at
  [$148 to $236 per acre-foot](https://water.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GSLBIP_AgOp_report_summary_final.pdf).
  At $236 that is $190 million a year (my arithmetic), to keep arsenic-bearing dust off 2.5 million
  people.
- The Colorado River shortage has a price. The 2027 Lower Basin cut is
  [1.25 million acre-feet](https://www.western-water.com/2026/08/21/the-decision-colorado-river-gets-2-year-plan-amid-record-lows/);
  at Carlsbad's seawater desalination price of about
  [$3,500 per acre-foot](https://mavensnotebook.com/2025/09/23/voice-of-san-diego-san-diegans-owe-a-desal-company-35-million-for-unmade-water/),
  replacing all of it costs $4.4 billion a year (my arithmetic). Cities can pay that. Alfalfa
  cannot.
- Louisiana cancelled the largest ecosystem restoration project in US history in July 2025 after
  [$618 million was already spent](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/07/louisiana-cancels-3-billion-coastal-restoration-project-funded-by-oil-spill-settlement/),
  even though BP oil spill settlement money was paying for it. Money was not the constraint.
- New capital cities get built and stay empty. Egypt's roughly
  [$59 billion](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1126491/opinion/international/a-new-capital-worthy-of-the-pharaohs-rises-in-egypt-but-at-what-price)
  New Administrative Capital has about 30,000 residents against a 6.5 million target
  ([ECES, 2026](https://eces.org.eg/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/ECONOMIC-LENS-Issue-20-En.pdf)).
  Saudi Arabia
  [halted The Line until after 2030](https://semafor.com/article/05/22/2026/saudis-neom-halts-work-on-the-line-until-after-2030).
- China passed
  [50,000 km of high-speed rail](https://railmarket.com/news/infrastructure/45532-china-s-high-speed-rail-network-exceeds-50-000-km-with-new-xi-an-yan-an-line)
  in December 2025 at a typical $17-21 million per km
  ([World Bank, 2014](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2014/07/10/cost-of-high-speed-rail-in-china-one-third-lower-than-in-other-countries)).
  California's 2026 plan prices Phase 1 at
  [$126.1 billion](https://www.railwayage.com/passenger/high-performance/chsra-issues-draft-2026-business-plan/),
  about $159 million per km for roughly 795 km (my arithmetic): eight times China.
- The biggest construction project now under way is barely discussed in the West: China's Yarlung
  Tsangpo hydropower scheme, started July 2025,
  [1.2 trillion yuan (about $168 billion)](https://foreignpolicy.com/2025/10/10/china-tibet-dam-hydropower-ecology/),
  60 GW,
  [300 TWh a year](https://rfa.org/english/tibet/2024/12/27/tibet-china-approves-mega-dam-construction/).
- A megaproject can be a subtraction. China's ten-year Yangtze fishing ban took
  [111,000 boats and 231,000 fishers off the river; fish biomass rose 209%](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/12022026/yangtze-river-fishing-ban-conservation/)
  in five years.
- Two famous ideas fail on physics, not money. An Earth space elevator needs a tether of
  [30-80 MYuri; Kevlar is 2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_elevator). Mars holds enough
  accessible CO2 for about
  [15 millibars](https://www.astronomy.com/science/theres-not-enough-co2-to-terraform-mars/) of
  extra pressure, and warming it needs something near a bar.

## Space Elevator: He Is Right, for a Better Reason

The mechanism is simple. A cable hangs from beyond geostationary orbit; everything above 35,786 km
wants to fly outward and holds up everything below. The cable has to carry its own weight through
Earth's gravity well, so its cross-section must grow exponentially toward GEO, and the exponent is
set by the material's strength-to-density ratio. Wikipedia's
[taper table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_elevator) gives the result: steel needs a cable
10³³ times thicker at GEO than at the ground, Kevlar 2.5 × 10⁸ times, a perfect single-wall carbon
nanotube 1.6 times. A workable tether needs a specific strength of 30-80 MYuri (MPa per kg/m³).
Kevlar is 2.5, Dyneema 3.7 and Zylon about 3.9
([Penoyre and Sandford, 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.09339)). Individual nanotubes test at the
right level, and a Tsinghua group reported centimetre-scale bundles at
[80 GPa in 2018](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/society/article/2170193/china-has-strongest-fibre-can-haul-160-elephants-and-space),
but strength collapses in bulk because one atomic defect cuts a tube's strength
[from about 100 GPa to 40](https://techradar.com/news/world-of-tech/there-s-a-flaw-in-our-plan-to-build-a-space-elevator-1323426),
and nobody has made a defect-free strand longer than about a metre. The requirement is tens of
thousands of kilometres.

The Moon version is buildable. The Moon's gravity well is roughly 18 times shallower than Earth's
(about 2.7 MJ/kg against 48.5, my arithmetic), so a Zylon cable needs a taper of about 2. Penoyre
and Sandford's "Spaceline" (2019) anchors on the Moon and hangs down toward Earth to near
geostationary height with existing fibers; an earlier LiftPort design fits a
[48-tonne system on one Falcon Heavy](https://spacesettlementprogress.com/tag/the-liftport-group/).
Nobody builds it because there is no cargo on the Moon to lift.

The competition is why "boring" is correct. A Falcon 9 lists at
[$74 million as of March 2026, about $3,250 per kg to low orbit, and Falcon Heavy about $1,520 per kg](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/06/21/how-accurate-were-elon-musks-falcon-9-and-starship-kg-claims/).
Starship V3
[first flew on 22 May 2026](https://space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/whats-next-for-spacexs-starship-v3-megarocket-after-its-historic-debut-flight);
its $100-200 per kg figure is a target, not a price. Elevator advocates promise about $220-500 per
kg to GEO. So the elevator needs a material that does not exist to match a price rockets are already
approaching. SpinLaunch, the centrifuge-launch company, has stopped being a launch company: its
effort is now the Meridian broadband constellation, flown on other people's rockets, which
[spun out as a separate firm in September 2026](https://satnews.com/2026/09/28/meridian-space-spins-out-of-spinlaunch-with-plan-for-250-satellite-reflect-array-constellation/).
The idea with real upside is the orbital ring (Birch, 1982): a hoop spun faster than orbital speed
carries stationary platforms 500 km up, with ordinary cables to the ground and no exotic materials.
My verdict: skip the elevator, or use it as a 200-word aside on what "blocked by physics" looks
like.

## Water

**The Colorado River is a pricing problem.** The seven states failed to agree, so Interior imposed
[two-year guidelines in August 2026](https://www.usbr.gov/newsroom/news-release/5283): a 1.25
million acre-foot Lower Basin cut for 2027, with the environmental review allowing up to 3 million.
Two engineered fixes exist. Reclamation's 2012 study priced a Mississippi pipeline at
[$1,700 per acre-foot for 600,000 acre-feet a year, 30 years to build](https://fronterasdesk.org/node/1685969),
which covers half the cut. Seawater desalination costs about $3,500 per acre-foot at Carlsbad and
[$1,500-2,100 at the newer Doheny plant](https://pbssocal.org/news-community/coastal-commission-pivots-on-desalination-plants-approves-project-in-orange-county),
against roughly
[$1,200 for treated imported water](https://iee.ucsb.edu/news-events/news/they-used-call-california-ocean-desalination-disaster-water-crisis-brings-new-look).
Replacing the whole 2027 cut by desalination costs $2.5-4.4 billion a year and the worst case about
$10 billion (my arithmetic). Southern California's cities could carry that. The reason nobody builds
it is that the cheapest acre-foot is always one bought from a farmer at a few hundred dollars. The
shortage is a fight over who stops irrigating, and the megaproject is a way to avoid having it.

**Great Salt Lake.** The lake sat at
[4,190.4 feet in early 2026](https://www.davisjournal.com/2026/01/26/561554/despite-significant-strides-great-salt-lake-ends-2025-at-third-lowest-water-level-on-record),
about 8 feet below the 4,198-foot minimum healthy level. Getting there by 2055 takes a sustained
800,000 acre-feet a year; state efforts delivered about
[400,000 acre-feet in total over 2021-2025](https://gardner.utah.edu/news/great-salt-lake-ends-2025-water-year-at-third-lowest-level-on-record-as-recovery-work-continue/).
If it fails, more than
[800 square miles of lakebed](https://goldschmidtabstracts.info/2025/32359.pdf) keep blowing dust
with arsenic above EPA screening levels onto the Wasatch Front
([Salt Lake Tribune, 2024](https://sltrib.com/news/2024/10/16/studies-great-salt-lake-dust-is)).
Owens Lake is the precedent for what dust control costs after the fact. The cure is the cheapest
water in this document: sprinkler upgrades at $148 per acre-foot, alfalfa leasing at $236. The Trump
administration has requested
[$1.0 billion for FY2027](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-04-30_R48928_5f2ebbd92a5fd3303ac566b63cfebf80252740a6.html).
The obstacle is that Utah water law gives conserved water to the next rights holder downstream
unless it is formally dedicated to the lake.

**The continental diversions.** NAWAPA is the type specimen:
[369 projects, a reservoir 805 km long, 75 million acre-feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Water_and_Power_Alliance),
with peaceful nuclear explosions proposed for excavation. It would sell irrigation water at
[$4 per acre-foot in 1964 dollars](https://sjsu.edu/faculty/watkins/NAWAPA.htm), which tells you the
economics: moving water a thousand miles to grow hay. Its descendants share the flaw. India's river
linking is the only one being built: the Ken-Betwa link,
[₹44,605 crore](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc2026821966401.pdf)
(about $5 billion), had its dam foundation about
[70% excavated by February 2026](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/ken-betwa-and-a-line-drawn-on-water/)
and floods part of a tiger reserve. The Dead Sea drops about
[a metre a year](https://ynetnews.com/environment/article/h1gjgg000d); Jordan abandoned the canal
for a
[$6 billion Aqaba-Amman desalination pipeline](https://www.greenprophet.com/2026/03/jordans-6-billion-aqaba-amman-desalination-project-from-the-red-sea-moves-forward/)
that gives it drinking water and the Dead Sea nothing.

**Yarlung Tsangpo.** This one is happening. Near Namcha Barwa the river drops
[2,000 m over 50 km](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3319175/china-building-worlds-biggest-hydropower-dam-why-india-worried)
around a hairpin bend. Bore tunnels across the bend and you get that head without a large reservoir:
60 GW, about three Three Gorges. Construction
[began 19 July 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medog_Hydropower_Station) with no published
environmental assessment, in one of the most seismically active places on Earth, upstream of India
and Bangladesh.

## Sea and Coast

**Atlantropa and the North Sea dam are the same idea with opposite intent.** Sörgel's plan worked on
real physics: the Mediterranean evaporates more than its rivers supply, and the difference flows in
through Gibraltar. Dam the strait and the sea falls,
[up to 200 m over more than a century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantropa), exposing new land
and creating a hydropower head. It would also strand every port and concentrate the salt, which is
roughly what happened naturally in the
[Messinian salinity crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messinian_salinity_crisis) six million
years ago. Groeskamp and Kjellsson's Northern European Enclosure Dam (2020) is a
[161 km dam from France to England plus 476 km from Scotland to Norway](https://www.deltaprogramma.nl/binaries/deltacommissaris/documenten/publicaties/2022/03/15/north-european-enclosure-dam---voortgangsrapportage-maart-2022/NEED+Summary+sheet.pdf)
at €250-500 billion, about
[0.1% of the GDP of the 14 protected countries for 20 years](https://www.dutchnews.nl/news/2020/02/dutch-mega-dam-scheme-raises-awareness-of-climate-crisis/).
The authors' point is that this is affordable, and that being able to afford the end of the North
Sea as a sea should frighten you. The Dutch are doing the sober version: their knowledge programme
finds the current dike strategy
[technically holds to at least 3 m of rise](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/17/3/437) (2025) and is
studying 2 m by 2100 and 5 m by 2150
([van Alphen et al., 2022](https://doaj.org/article/8fb21db5ee1b4b2a8289356bbd8f127c)).

**MOSE is the preview.** Venice's barrier was designed for occasional closures. It was raised
[100 times in its first four years at about €200,000 each](https://campaignforalivingvenice.org/2025/01/11/mose-raised-100-times-in-the-first-four-years-at-a-cost-of-twenty-million-euros/),
and [26 times in three weeks](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/02/11/mose-venezia-molto-attivo/) in
early 2026. A 2026 study projects the lagoon could be shut off
[248 days a year by century's end](https://www.greenme.it/ambiente/nuovo-studio-rivela-il-lato-oscuro-del-mose-a-fine-secolo-potrebbe-isolare-venezia-dal-mare-per-248-giorni-lanno-con-conseguenze-nefaste-per-la-laguna/).
A barrier that closes most days is a dam, and the lagoon depends on the tide to flush it. Every
surge barrier, New York's included, is on this curve.

**Seabed curtains.** Thwaites holds [65 cm of sea level](https://thwaitesglacier.org/node/834). It
melts from below because warm, salty, dense water flows along seabed troughs to the grounding line.
A flexible curtain anchored to the seabed blocks that bottom layer while letting surface water and
icebergs pass above it. Keefer, Wolovick and Moore (2023) price
[80 km of curtain in 600 m of water at $40-80 billion plus $1-2 billion a year](https://arcticcentre.ulapland.fi/johnmoore/pdfs/Wolovick_Pnasnexus2023.pdf).
Against that, defending the world's coasts this century costs
[$2.7-9.6 trillion](https://www.greenpolicyplatform.org/research/global-investment-costs-coastal-defence-through-21st-century)
([Nicholls et al., 2019](https://www.greenpolicyplatform.org/research/global-investment-costs-coastal-defence-through-21st-century)).
The objection came in force in September 2025, when a group of polar scientists led by Martin
Siegert concluded in Frontiers in Science that
[no polar geoengineering scheme has been tested and all would cost over $10 billion and risk harm](https://www.frontiersin.org/news/2025/09/09/polar-geoengineering-ideas-not-help-harm).
The lead I was given credits the $40-80 billion figure to Wolovick and Moore 2018; that paper
proposed solid sills, and the curtain costing is the 2023 paper.

## Restoration

**Dam removal is the best-evidenced megaproject category of the decade.** The Klamath's four dams
came out because relicensing them with fish ladders would have
[cost PacifiCorp's customers more than removal](https://commondreams.org/newswire/2010/09/17/its-official-removing-klamath-dams-saves-money-power-customers).
The final cofferdam was dismantled in
[October 2024](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/final-step-klamath-river-dam-removal-opens-path-returning-salmon)
and salmon passed the sites within days. In March 2026 the Klamath Tribes documented the
[first Chinook hatched above Upper Klamath Lake in a century](https://www.klcc.org/environment/2026-03-25/chinook-salmon-are-hatching-in-upper-klamath-for-first-time-in-a-century).
The caveat: 2026 brought
[drought and warm water](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/27082026/salmon-return-to-klamath-river-after-dam-removals/),
and recovery will not be linear. The Elwha, a decade on, is the sober comparison. The river's run
was about
[392,000 fish before the dams and 3,000 after](https://www.yahoo.com/news/1-2-dams-down-fish-return-elwha-river-131532208--finance.html).
The 2025 survey counted
[867 Chinook redds and a record 11,607 pink salmon](https://www.elwha.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Spatial-Spawning-Distribution-of-Elwha-Chinook-2012-2025.pdf),
and steelhead numbers rose, wild fish included, but Chinook are
[still mostly hatchery fish](https://doaj.org/article/cffb6f49e6dd482b91cdd83db54fcc66) (2024).

**The two that are not happening.** Replacing the Lower Snake dams' power, barging and irrigation
costs
[$10.3-27.2 billion](https://lmtribune.com/northwest/report-cost-to-replace-dams-between-103b-and-272b-be6785f9)
(Murray-Inslee report, 2022). The Trump administration withdrew from the Biden-era agreement in
2025, litigation
[resumed that October](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2025/10/16/renewed-legal-battle-ramps-up-over-columbia-basin-dams-and-salmon/),
and in February 2026 a judge ordered
[more spill but no drawdown](https://www.eenews.net/articles/flows-must-favor-fish-in-pacific-northwest-dams-judge-rules).
Hetch Hetchy is a second Yosemite Valley under San Francisco's drinking water. Reagan's Interior
Secretary Donald Hodel
[proposed draining it in 1987](https://www.spur.org/news/2012-09-04/californias-water-wars-three-decades-same-issues);
California's 2006 study put full restoration at
[$3-10 billion](https://www.nationalparkstraveler.org/2006/07/restoring-hetch-hetchy-pricey-proposition),
while Environmental Defense priced replacing the water and power at
[under $2 billion](https://www.edf.org/media/environmental-defense-releases-hetch-hetchy-valley-study).
In 2012 San Franciscans voted
[77% to 23%](https://ballotpedia.org/San_Francisco_Hetch_Hetchy_Reservoir_Initiative,_Proposition_F_%28November_2012%29)
against spending $8 million to study it.

**Mid-Barataria.** The Mississippi built Louisiana by flooding; levees stopped the flooding and the
delta has been sinking since. The diversion was a gated cut in the levee to let sediment-laden river
water rebuild marsh. It was permitted, under construction and funded when Governor Landry paused it
in April 2025 and
[cancelled it on 17 July](https://www.enr.com/articles/61064-louisiana-pulls-plug-on-3b-sediment-diversion-project).
The stated reasons were cost growth past $3 billion and a claim the basin will be open water by 2070
anyway. The operative reason is that fresh water moves the oyster and shrimp grounds:
[oystermen celebrated](https://www.yahoo.com/news/environmentalists-lament-while-oystermen-celebrate-215525001.html).
The state now proposes a smaller diversion plus dredging, which builds land once and does not
sustain it. It is the cleanest case here of a few hundred concentrated losers beating a diffuse
benefit to millions
([Audubon, 2025](https://www.audubon.org/news/louisiana-pulls-plug-nations-largest-ecosystem-restoration-project)).

**Everglades and Kissimmee.** CERP was authorized in 2000 at $7.8 billion (the Congressional
Research Service says $8.2 billion) and is now $23 billion. What is done: the C-43 and C-44
reservoirs, and Picayune Strand, completed
[January 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/FLDEP/bulletins/4068a06). The keystone, the
240,000 acre-foot EAA reservoir, has been pulled forward
[from 2034 to 2029](https://sfwmd.gov/eaareservoir). The Kissimmee, just upstream, finished in 2021:
the Corps had straightened it into a canal in the 1960s, then spent three decades putting the bends
back. Wading birds
[exceeded the restoration target](https://sfer-docs.sfwmd.gov/2020_sfer_final/v1/chapters/v1_ch9.pdf)
(2020).

**Seoul and the Yangtze.** Seoul tore out an elevated freeway and opened a 5.8 km stream in 2005 for
[386 billion won, $323 million](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/20151001/cheonggye-stream-restoration-marks-10th-year),
≈ $540 million today. It draws about
[50,000-60,000 visitors a day and runs 3.3-5.9°C cooler](https://landscapeperformance.org/case-study-briefs/cheonggyecheon-stream-restoration)
than nearby streets. Purists note the water is pumped from the Han. On the Yangtze, the finless
porpoise count rose from 1,249 in 2022 to 1,426 in 2025. Tulare Lake made the same point by
accident: in 2023 snowmelt refilled
[about 100,000 acres](https://kmph.com/news/nation-world/ghost-lake-tulare-enormous-ghst-reemerges-after-vanishing-19th-century-swallows-94k-acres-cincinnati-vengeance-golden-state-private-farmland-sweeping-eco-life-indigenous-population-culture-expense-expanse-tachi-yokut-tribe-conditions-extreme-sierra)
of California farmland, waterbirds returned, and a year later it was drained back to under 3,000
acres.

**Rewilding.** American Prairie holds about
[170,000 acres owned plus roughly 440,000-470,000 leased](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/blm-yanks-american-prairie-bison-grazing-leases),
a fifth of its goal. Its model depends on federal grazing leases, and in 2026 the Bureau of Land
Management
[cancelled six bison permits](https://washingtonstatestandard.com/2026/05/13/repub/feds-cancel-conservation-groups-montana-bison-grazing-leases/)
on the theory that grazing law covers only livestock raised for production; a judge has
[let the bison stay](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/court-says-american-prairie-bison-can-stay-now)
for now. Colorado's wolves:
[14 of 25 translocated animals were dead by March 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/03/13/colorado-wolf-death-number-14/),
the state has
[paused releases](https://coloradosun.com/2026/01/21/colorado-parks-wildlife-no-new-wolves-2026/),
and the minimum count is 32 wolves in four packs. Europe is the success: free-roaming bison went
from
[2,579 to about 7,000 in a decade](https://euronews.com/2026/04/08/from-biodiversity-to-carbon-capture-saving-bison-from-extinction-has-brought-big-benefits-),
and beavers from 1,200 animals to at least 1.5 million
([Halley et al., 2020](https://beavertrust.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Halley_et_al-2020-Mammal_Review.pdf)).

## Planet-Scale and Space

**Asteroid deflection.** DART hit at 6 km/s and the period change was nearly five times what the
spacecraft's momentum alone would give, because ejecta blasted off the rubble-pile surface acted as
a rocket exhaust. That multiplier is what Hera is there to pin down: it will measure Dimorphos's
mass and image the crater, which
[DART's companion cubesat could not resolve](https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2026/hera-spots-its-target-a-first-glimpse-into-the-study-of-the-didymos-binary-asteroid-system).
Hera arrived a month earlier than planned. If Robbie's post runs after 25 November, it can use the
close-up images.

**Where the energy scale wins.** A mature hurricane releases heat at about
[200 times the world's electrical generating capacity](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/hrd/tcfaq/D7.html).
Project Stormfury (1962-1983) seeded eyewalls with silver iodide and saw them weaken, then learned
that
[unseeded hurricanes do the same thing on their own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Stormfury).
Bubble curtains that lift cold water
([OceanTherm](https://www.space.com/bubble-curtain-could-reduce-hurricane-intensity)) would have to
cool thousands of square kilometres ahead of a moving storm. Earthquakes are similar. The USGS did
control small quakes at Rangely, Colorado, in 1969-1973 by raising and lowering fluid pressure in an
oil field
([Raleigh et al., 1976](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/experiment-earthquake-control-rangely-colorado)).
But each magnitude unit is about 32 times the energy, so bleeding off a magnitude 8 takes roughly
32,000 magnitude 5s (my arithmetic), any of which could trigger the big one. The serious volcano
intervention is modest and works: Iceland's walls
[diverted lava from Grindavík, the Blue Lagoon and the Svartsengi power plant](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/iceland-builds-defences-for-a-future-of-increased-volcanic-activity/72094896),
though one fissure opened inside the line and burned houses. Mars is the limiting case: Jakosky and
Edwards (2018) inventoried the polar caps, regolith and carbonates and found that releasing
everything reachable gives at most
[7% of Earth's pressure](https://www.sci.news/space/mars-terraforming-06258.html), most of it not
reachable at all
([paper](https://lasp.colorado.edu/maven/files/2018/08/Inventory-of-CO2-available-for-terraforming-Mars.pdf)).

**Sunshade and space solar.** Angel's 2006 design is 16 trillion one-gram flyers at L1,
[20 million tonnes for a few trillion dollars](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1859907),
and assumes electromagnetic launch at $20 a pound. Szapudi (2023) cut the launched mass to
[35,000 tonnes](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/news/article.php?aId=12706) by tethering the shield to a
captured asteroid counterweight, which swaps a launch problem for an asteroid-capture problem. Space
solar has been shown to work in principle: Caltech's MAPLE
[beamed detectable power to a Pasadena rooftop in 2023](https://www.space.com/space-solar-power-satellite-beams-energy-1st-time).
NASA's 2024 study priced two designs at
[$0.61 and $1.59 per kWh against $0.02-0.05 for ground renewables](https://spacenews.com/nasa-report-offers-pessimistic-take-on-space-based-solar-power/),
with launch at 71-77% of cost; only stacking every optimistic assumption reaches $0.03.

## Cities and Land

New cities fail on demand, not construction. The Line's 2030 population target is now
[at most 100,000](https://semafor.com/article/05/22/2026/saudis-neom-halts-work-on-the-line-until-after-2030)
against an original design for 9 million. Nusantara's budget fell from
[43.4 trillion rupiah in 2024 to 6.3 trillion in 2026](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/09/07/indonesia039s-delayed-new-capital-risks-039white-elephant039-status)
and about 1,000 authority staff live there. Land reclamation works when the land is worth more than
the fill: Lynetteholm takes Copenhagen's construction spoil and doubles as a storm barrier. Tunnels
follow the same rule. Fehmarnbelt (18 km, 89 immersed elements,
[about €7.4 billion](https://transport-online.de/en/news/fehmarnbelt-how-work-tunnel-progressing-189194.html))
sank its first element in May 2026 and [will not open before 2031](https://heise.de/-10662632).
Gibraltar was judged feasible in 2025 at
[over €8.5 billion](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/10/265853/spain-confirms-feasibility-of-e8-5-billion-morocco-europe-tunnel/).
The Japan-Korea tunnel fails Korea's own cost-benefit test
[by more than half](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Korea_Undersea_Tunnel), and the
Bering tunnel resurfaced in October 2025 as a Kremlin envoy's
[$8 billion "Putin-Trump tunnel"](https://www.rcinet.ca/eye-on-the-arctic/2025/10/17/kremlin-envoy-proposes-putin-trump-tunnel-to-link-arctic-russia-alaska/)
connecting two places with no railways.

## Why Don't We?

**Cost disease.** The Transit Costs Project's database of
[755 lines in 60 countries averages $252 million per km](https://transitcosts.com/new-data/). New
York's Second Avenue Subway Phase 1 came in at
[8 to 12 times the project's composite baseline](https://pedestrianobservations.com/2022/10/24/the-transit-costs-project-conclusion-is-out/);
Phase 2 is budgeted near
[$4 billion a mile](https://www.vitalcitynyc.org/articles/why-it-costs-4-billion-per-mile-of-subway-track).
The project's diagnosis is specific: oversized stations, agencies that outsource design to
consultants, and overstaffing. The same disease shows in everything above that is built in the US:
CERP tripled, California HSR quadrupled from the $33 billion voters approved, and PG&E buries a mile
of distribution line for $3.1 million.

**Permitting.** The median federal environmental impact statement took
[2.4 years in 2021-2024](https://ceq.doe.gov/docs/nepa-practice/CEQ_EIS_Timeline_Report_2025-1-13.pdf)
(CEQ, 2025). The tail is the problem: California HSR began environmental work on one segment in 2009
and got its last San Francisco-Los Angeles clearance in
[June 2024](https://www.metro-magazine.com/news/californias-high-speed-rail-project-clears-final-hurdle-to-connect-s-f-to-l-a).
Restoration is not exempt, since removing a dam needs the same reviews as building one. California
exempted infill housing from CEQA in
[June 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/california-legislature-enacts-major-ceqa-reforms-for-housing-rich);
the federal SPEED Act
[passed the House 221-196 in December 2025](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49485.html) and
is still in the Senate.

**Sorting by Robbie's premise.**

- Blocked by physics: Earth space elevator, terraforming Mars, hurricane control, earthquake
  control. Money does not help.
- Blocked by money, meaning the benefit is smaller than the cost: space solar, the Mississippi
  pipeline, NAWAPA, nationwide undergrounding, the Japan-Korea and Bering tunnels, the L1 sunshade.
  "Unlimited money" builds these and it would be a waste.
- Blocked by will: Great Salt Lake, Mid-Barataria, the Lower Snake dams, the Colorado River
  settlement, US high-speed rail, the supergrid, American Prairie. These pass on the numbers and
  stall on a small group with a property right or a veto: irrigators, oystermen, barge operators,
  77% of San Francisco.
- Blocked by governance across borders: Transaqua, Sibaral, the Bering dam, Thwaites curtains
  (Antarctic Treaty), any sunshade.
- Should stay blocked because they are irreversible: Atlantropa, the North Sea dam, the GRAND Canal.

The projects that got done share one feature. Either the loser was bought out or the loser preferred
it: PacifiCorp wanted out of the Klamath dams, Seoul negotiated with and relocated the street's
merchants (unverified), China resettled 231,000 fishers. "Political will" in practice means someone
designed the compensation.

1. **Great Salt Lake.** $150-250 per acre-foot stands between Salt Lake City and arsenic dust, and
   it is failing on water-rights plumbing.
2. **Mid-Barataria.** The largest US restoration project, fully funded by BP, killed after $618
   million. The best "why don't we" case study.
3. **Klamath vs. Elwha.** Removal as the owner's cheapest option; salmon back in days; the honest
   ten-year picture next door.
4. **The Colorado River priced in desalination.** A $4-10 billion a year problem dressed as an
   existential one.
5. **Yangtze fishing ban.** A megaproject made of not doing something, with a 209% result.
6. **Yarlung Tsangpo.** $168 billion, three Three Gorges, started last year, almost unknown.
7. **MOSE becoming a dam.** Built for rare closures, raised 26 times in three weeks; the future of
   every surge barrier.
8. **Atlantropa and the North Sea dam.** A utopian crank plan and a deliberate warning, mechanically
   identical.
9. **Thwaites curtains.** $40-80 billion against trillions, and a scientific community that does not
   want it tested.
10. **Empty capitals.** $59 billion for 30,000 residents; The Line halted. Concrete is the easy
    part.

Runners-up: Iceland's $44 million lava walls, the lunar elevator, Hera's images due 25 November.
