# Done Biology and Terminology

Research date: 2026-11-17. Lane: (A) what to call this, (B) large-scale deliberate biological
interventions already carried out.

## Headline Findings

- **The screwworm is back in the US.** First case June 3, 2026 (a calf in Zavala County, Texas),
  [45 detections by August 10](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49124.html),
  [113 by November 4](https://hpj.com/2026/11/05/usda-reports-61-new-world-screwworm-cases-in-30-day-period/),
  [128 by November 14](https://www.natureworldnews.com/articles/73884/20261117/screwworm-confirmed-tucson-area-horse-no-known-travel-history-us-cases-climb-128.htm)
  across Texas, New Mexico and Arizona. Over 60% of them were confirmed after October 1.
- **The barrier that failed cost about $20 million a year.** APHIS spent
  [roughly $20 million a year](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49124.html) holding the line
  in Panama, against a benefit to US producers of
  [$796 million a year in 1996 dollars](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/nws-historical-economic-impact.pdf).
  After it broke, APHIS obligated
  [about $999 million between FY2024 and May 2026](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49124.html),
  which is about 50 years of barrier budget.
- **"Malaria gone from South America" is wrong.** The Americas reported
  [536,700 cases in 2024](https://www.paho.org/en/topics/malaria), up 11% since 2015, and WHO counts
  [16 endemic countries](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240117822) in the region. Only
  three South American countries are WHO-certified malaria-free: Paraguay (2018), Argentina (2019),
  Suriname (2025).
- **By the public-health definitions, the screwworm was never eradicated.** It was eliminated
  regionally, which by definition requires continued intervention. Only two diseases have ever been
  eradicated: smallpox and rinderpest.
- **Aedes aegypti was cleared from
  [18 countries by 1962](https://iris.paho.org/handle/10665.2/14350)** and then reinfested
  practically all of them. It is the vector of today's dengue.
- **The cheapest large intervention on record:**
  [514 beetles and about $1,500](https://biocontrol.entomology.cornell.edu/predators/Rodolia.php)
  saved California citrus in 1889.
- **China's sparrow campaign killed people.** A 2024 NBER paper attributes
  [about 1.9 million famine deaths](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f196680.pdf) to it.
- **Kudzu is a smaller backfire than its legend.** The Forest Service finds it on
  [about 227,000 acres of forest](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/true-story-kudzu-vine-ate-south-180956325/),
  not the millions usually quoted.
- **Wild mammals are 5% of mammal biomass.** Livestock and pets are 59%, humans 36%
  ([Greenspoon et al., 2023, via Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/wild-mammals-birds-biomass)).

### Eradication, Elimination, Control

These are precise terms, set at the 1997 Dahlem Workshop and published by
[Dowdle (1998)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/su48a7.htm):

| Level                    | Definition                                                                                             | Example            |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Control                  | Reduction of incidence to a locally acceptable level; continued intervention required                  | Diarrhoeal disease |
| Elimination of disease   | Zero incidence of disease in a defined area; continued intervention required                           | Neonatal tetanus   |
| Elimination of infection | Zero incidence of infection in a defined area; continued measures to prevent re-establishment required | Measles, polio     |
| Eradication              | Permanent reduction to zero of worldwide incidence; intervention no longer needed                      | Smallpox           |
| Extinction               | Agent no longer exists in nature or the laboratory                                                     | None               |

WHO's malaria version: elimination is the
[interruption of local transmission in a defined area](https://www.afro.who.int/node/18712),
certified after three consecutive years of zero indigenous cases plus a surveillance system able to
prevent re-establishment. A country loses certification after three or more indigenous cases of one
species in one focus for three consecutive years.

The distinction that matters: **elimination has a running cost forever, eradication does not.** USDA
says the screwworm was "eradicated" from North America. By Dahlem definitions it was eliminated,
because the species lives on in South America and the result only held while sterile flies kept
falling on Panama. That is exactly the failure that happened.

### Recommendation

Least-bad umbrella: **"large-scale environmental modification"** (or "deliberate environmental
modification"). It is the only term whose formal definition names biota, lithosphere, hydrosphere
and atmosphere together, so it covers levees, eradicated pests and stratospheric sulfur. Its costs:
it is clunky, and the treaty context is military.

For a blog title, "geoengineering" with a one-sentence redefinition up front ("I mean any deliberate
large-scale change to the physical or living world, not just climate") is fine, as long as the post
says the word normally means climate. "Terraforming Earth" is the punchier alternative.

Climbable trees in a city park fail every large-scale term on scale. The agreed understandings
attached to ENMOD define "widespread" as several hundred square kilometres (unverified). The park is
landscape design. Only "niche construction" includes it, and that term also includes a termite
mound.

## B. The Record

### New World Screwworm

**The pest.** _Cochliomyia hominivorax_ lays eggs on the edge of open wounds in warm-blooded
animals; the larvae eat living flesh. In 1935 it killed
[180,000 livestock in under half of Texas's counties](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/06/25/texas-screwworm-history-eradication/).

**How the sterile insect technique works.** Edward Knipling and Raymond Bushland, USDA
entomologists, noticed that
[the female mates only once](https://www.nal.usda.gov/exhibits/speccoll/exhibits/show/stop-screwworms--selections-fr/introduction)
in her life while males mate repeatedly. If she mates with a sterile male, her entire lifetime egg
output is dead. So: mass-rear flies in a factory, sterilize the pupae with radiation at a dose that
breaks the germline but leaves them able to fly and court, and drop them from aircraft in numbers
that swamp wild males. At 9 sterile males per wild male, 90% of matings produce nothing and the next
generation is a tenth the size. Release the same number again and the ratio is 90 to 1. The method
gets more effective as the target gets rarer, the opposite of insecticide, where the last survivors
are the hardest to find and the most resistant. With a generation time of
[about 21 days](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screwworm), collapse takes months.

**The barrier.** The Darién Gap is the narrowest defensible line between an infested continent and a
free one. COPEG released about
[20 million sterile flies a week](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49124.html) over it. APHIS
paid 90%, about $20 million a year since FY2012 (my brief said $15 million; CRS says $20 million).
Against that: producer benefits of
[$796 million a year and $2.8 billion to the wider economy](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/nws-historical-economic-impact.pdf)
(1996 dollars). APHIS estimates a 1976-scale outbreak today would cost Texas producers
[$732 million a year](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/nws-historical-economic-impact.pdf).

government
[released its billionth sterile fly over Texas](https://www.wect.com/2026/09/07/government-releases-billions-flies-heres-why/)

USDA began hiring its 228 staff on November 10, 2026. A converted plant at Metapa, Mexico should add
60 to 100 million a week.

### Malaria

**The premise is wrong.** WHO-certified malaria-free countries in the Americas, from the
[WHO register](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/elimination/countries-and-territories-certified-malaria-free-by-who):

| Country              | Certified |
| -------------------- | --------- |
| Grenada, Saint Lucia | 1962      |
| Trinidad and Tobago  | 1965      |
| Dominica, Jamaica    | 1966      |
| United States        | 1970      |
| Cuba                 | 1973      |
| Paraguay             | 2018      |
| Argentina            | 2019      |
| El Salvador          | 2021      |
| Belize               | 2023      |
| Suriname             | 2025      |

Malaria remains endemic in 16 countries. Of the 2024 total,
[Colombia had 26.8%, Brazil 24.4%, Venezuela 16.7% and Haiti 13.8%](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240117822).
About [68% is _P. vivax_](https://www.paho.org/en/topics/malaria), which hides dormant in the liver
and relapses, and reported deaths were about 136. So the true statement is "malaria is gone from
North America, most of the Caribbean, and the southern cone".

**United States.** In 1933 malaria affected
[30% of the population of the Tennessee Valley](https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/history/index.html). The
TVA's dams could have made it worse; instead engineers
[dropped reservoir levels about a foot each week](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081005195714/https:/www.tva.gov/environment/reports/ros_eis/4-12_vector_control.pdf)
in mosquito season, stranding larvae on the shore. In 1942 the Public Health Service set up the
Office of Malaria Control in War Areas in Atlanta, because that is where the malaria and the
training bases were. It became the CDC in 1946, which is why the CDC is in Atlanta. The National
Malaria Eradication Program started July 1, 1947 across 13 states, sprayed DDT on the inside walls
of rural houses
[4.65 million times by the end of 1949](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121007154452/http://www.cdc.gov/malaria/about/history/elimination_us.html),
and saw reported cases fall from 15,000 (1947) to 2,000 (1950). Malaria was considered eliminated
in 1951.

The caveat: historian Margaret Humphreys titled her 1996 paper
["Kicking a Dying Dog"](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/645919). Malaria was collapsing before
DDT, through drainage, window screens, and the rural poor moving to town. A
[2013 study of Alabama counties](https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/study-malaria-decline-us-could-affect-approach-malaria-epidemic)
credits drainage and local health departments over migration. Either way, DDT finished a job mostly
done.

**Southern Europe.** Spain was certified in 1964, Italy in 1970, Portugal in 1973. The Rockefeller
Foundation's Sardinia Project (1946 to 1950) put
[267 tonnes of DDT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2819864) on the island trying to
exterminate the mosquito _Anopheles labranchiae_. It failed at that, and malaria vanished anyway
(Tognotti, 2009). Deaths had already fallen from
[3,800 in 1918 to 138 in 1940](https://www.carnegiecouncil.org/publications/articles_papers_reports/5117).
The WHO European Region reported
[zero indigenous cases in 2015](https://www.who.int/news/item/07-10-2016-kyrgyzstan-receives-who-certification-of-malaria-elimination).

**The Global Malaria Eradication Programme, 1955 to 1969.** The plan was a race: spray houses with
DDT hard enough to stop transmission for a few years, let the parasite die out of the human
population, and finish before mosquitoes evolved resistance,
[first seen in Greece in 1951](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000412).
It worked on islands and in temperate margins, and was never seriously tried in tropical Africa. The
US supplied over 85% of the special fund and stopped in 1963 (Nájera et al., 2011). Sri Lanka is the
standard parable:
[17 cases in 1963](https://malariajournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1475-2875-11-S1-O10),
spraying wound down, about 538,000 cases in 1969.

### Rewilding That Already Happened

**Wolves, Yellowstone.**
[41 wolves released 1995 to 1997](https://home.nps.gov/articles/wolf-restoration-in-yellowstone-reintroduction-to-recovery.htm);
[about 109 in nine packs](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2025/01/11/30-years-of-yellowstone-wolves-feds-released-them-in-1995-without-telling-wyoming)
at the end of 2024. The northern elk herd fell from
[19,045 in 1994](https://www.nationalparkstraveler.org/2011/01/annual-count-shows-huge-decline-yellowstone-national-park-elk-herd-how-accurate-it7458)
to 3,915 in 2013. The popular story (wolves scare elk, willows regrow, beavers return, rivers change
course) is much weaker than the viral video.
[Hobbs et al. (2024)](https://esa.org/blog/2024/02/07/apex-predators-not-a-quick-fix-for-restoring-ecosystems-20-year-csu-study-finds/)
ran a 20-year experiment with fences and artificial beaver dams: willows recover only with both less
browsing and a higher water table. With beavers gone, streams cut down into their beds, and the
system settled into a stable grassland state that wolves alone do not reverse. Elk also fell because
of cougars, grizzlies, hunters outside the park and drought, and bison now browse where elk did. A
2025 paper claiming a strong cascade drew a
[formal comment titled "Flawed analysis invalidates claim"](https://www.livescience.com/animals/land-mammals/did-reintroducing-wolves-to-yellowstone-really-cause-an-ecological-cascade).
Fair summary: real, weak, patchy.

**Beavers.** From a pre-contact
[60 to 400 million](https://canadiangeographic.ca/articles/rethinking-the-beaver/) (a guess) to
[about 100,000 by 1900](https://www.encountersnorth.org/beaver-summary/2017/8/2/beaver-ecology), now
10 to 15 million, through trapping limits and translocation.

**Bison.** Tens of millions to under 1,000 by the 1890s. Today about
[30,000 in conservation herds](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/nature/bison.htm), 5,300 of them in
Yellowstone; the other several hundred thousand are livestock.

**California condor.** 22 birds in 1982, all in captivity by 1987. At the end of 2025:
[607 alive, 392 in the wild](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2026-02/2025-california-condor-population-status_508-compliant.pdf).
Lead from bullet fragments in carcasses caused 50% of deaths with a known cause, so the population
is not self-sustaining.

### The Green Revolution and Haber-Bosch

My reading of Robbie's line: disease control (DDT, vaccines, sanitation) cut death rates, population
boomed, and the Green Revolution (dwarf wheat and rice bred to turn heavy nitrogen into grain) was
the intervention needed to feed the result. It is also the largest deliberate modification of the
biosphere so far.

- Synthetic nitrogen fed
  [48% of humanity in 2008](https://www.iai.int/admin/site/sites/default/files/uploads/2008.Erisman-et-al_NatureGeo.pdf)
  (Erisman et al., 2008). Smil's 2001 version: about
  [2.5 billion people exist because the nitrogen in their proteins came from Haber-Bosch](https://evolution-outreach.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1007/s12052-009-0143-2).
  "Half the nitrogen in your body is synthetic" is the popular gloss.
- Humans directly use [over 70% of ice-free land](https://www.ipcc.ch/srccl/chapter/spm/) (IPCC,
  2019):
  [12 to 14% cropland, 37% grazing, 22% managed forest](https://www.carbonbrief.org/in-depth-qa-the-ipccs-special-report-on-climate-change-and-land).
  [Half of habitable land is farmed](https://ourworldindata.org/global-land-for-agriculture).
- Mammal biomass is
  [59% livestock and pets, 36% humans, 5% wild](https://ourworldindata.org/wild-mammals-birds-biomass).
  Cattle alone are 38%. Poultry are 71% of bird biomass.

## Synthesis: When Does It Work?

1. **Specialists work, generalists backfire.** Every success exploited one narrow fact: a female
   that mates once, a virus with only human hosts, a beetle that eats one scale, a mosquito that
   breeds in water jars. Every introduced generalist predator (toad, mongoose, _Compsilura_) missed
   its target and ate something else.
2. **Removing beats adding.** A failed removal is reversible: the pest returns and you are back
   where you started, minus the money. A failed introduction is permanent. No one will ever remove
   200 million toads.
3. **Removing a recent invader or a pathogen is safe; removing a native generalist is not.**
   Sparrows had a job nobody had measured.
4. **Islands and chokepoints.** Curaçao, Unguja, South Georgia, Santiago. The screwworm campaign
   worked because North America narrows to an isthmus. _Aedes aegypti_ and the malaria programme had
   no line to hold.
5. **Elimination decays, eradication does not.** Anything short of global extinction needs a budget
   forever, and success removes the constituency for that budget. Aedes after 1962, Sri Lanka after
   1963, Panama after 2022.
6. **Chemical and viral tools evolve away.** DDT resistance appeared within about five years of
   first use, and myxoma virus attenuated within a decade. Sterile males are still working after 70
   years.
7. **The disasters were untested, not unforeseeable.** No feeding trial for toads or mongooses.
   Modern biocontrol tests host range first.
8. **The winners are absurdly cheap.** $1,500 for California citrus, $300 million for smallpox, $20
   million a year for a continent free of screwworm.

## Best Anecdotes

- **Mata Hari goats.** Goats are gregarious, so a radio-collared "Judas goat" leads the helicopter
  to the last holdouts. Project Isabela improved on this:
  [sterilized females given hormone implants](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092746) to
  keep them permanently in heat, so males came to them. Over 700 were deployed, and 266 were left on
  the island as living alarms.
- **Geronimo the beaver.** In 1948 Idaho Fish and Game
  [parachuted 76 beavers](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/why-beavers-were-parachuted-into-the-idaho-wilderness)
  into roadless wilderness in boxes built to spring open on landing. The design was tested by
  dropping one old male, Geronimo, over and over. 75 survived.
- **The beetle and the DDT.** Fifty-odd years after 514 vedalia beetles saved citrus, growers
  sprayed DDT, killed the beetles, and the scale came back. They ended up
  [paying $1 a beetle](https://biocontrol.entomology.cornell.edu/predators/Rodolia.php) to restock.
- **The 79-year fungus.** A Japanese fungus was released against gypsy moths near Boston in
  [1910 and 1911, failed, was retried in 1985 and failed](https://northernwoodlands.org/articles/article/entomophaga-maimaiga).
  In June 1989 it appeared from nowhere in Connecticut, killing
  [60 to 88% of caterpillars](https://biocontrol.entomology.cornell.edu/pathogens/entomophagamaimaiga.php).
  Nobody knows where it came from.
- **Screwworms in Libya.** In 1988 the fly turned up in Libya, its first foothold outside the
  Americas. The FAO [flew sterile flies from Mexico](https://www.fao.org/4/U4220T/u4220T04.htm),
  over 600 million by mid-1991, and it was gone that year.
- **The cattle ration.** The Darién barrier's fly budget was set for 64,000 cattle. Ranching doubled
  the herd and nobody doubled the flies.

## Could Not Verify

- **Polio in Afghanistan.** Secondary sources give 21 cases for 2025 (52 worldwide with Pakistan's
  31). I could not confirm this on the GPEI site, and one November 2026 report of 50 Afghan cases
  this year looks wrong against September's 23.
- **Guinea worm 2026 year-to-date.** No figure found. The latest is 10 provisional human cases
  for 2025.
- **"Half the nitrogen in your body."** I found it only in popular sources. The peer-reviewed claims
  are about share of population fed.
- **Vedalia "129 beetles".** Cornell says 514 in total; 129 may be the first shipment.
- **US _Aedes aegypti_ programme.** It ran from about 1964 to 1969 and was dropped; I could not
  source its cost.
- **Sparrow imports.** The claim that China imported 250,000 sparrows from the USSR afterwards
  traces only to Wikipedia. The 2 billion kill count is a government propaganda figure.
- **Cassava: "20 million lives saved".** A prize-committee estimate with no study behind it.
- **Myxomatosis kill rates** (over 99% at first) and the fall from 600 million to 100 million
  rabbits: not found in a source I could open.
- **DDE mechanism.** How DDE thins shells at the biochemical level is not settled in the sources I
  found.
- **Chile and Uruguay** have no malaria but are not on the certified list; I believe WHO lists them
  as places where it disappeared without a programme.
- **A third "beetle" candidate:** the Asian lady beetle (_Harmonia axyridis_), released as
  biocontrol in the US and now an invasive nuisance. Not researched.
- **French deaths at Panama.** 22,000 is an estimate from incomplete records; one source gives about
  7,500.
- **Texas plant schedule.** Sources give first output anywhere from mid-2027 to November 2027, and
  reported cost as either $726 million obligated or $750 million.
