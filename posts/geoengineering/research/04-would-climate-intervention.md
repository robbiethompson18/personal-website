# 04: Would We Do Climate Intervention? (Sulfur in the Stratosphere and Its Rivals)

Research notes, compiled 2026-11-17. Lane: deliberate climate intervention, centered on
stratospheric aerosol injection (SAI). "Mt" is million metric tons (1 Mt = 1 Tg). Sulfur masses are
SO2 unless marked S (1 t of S burns to 2 t of SO2). Figures marked "my arithmetic" are derived here
from sourced inputs.

## Headline Findings

- **Cooling the planet 1 °C with stratospheric sulfur costs about $18 billion a year**
  ([Smith 2020](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aba7e7)), under 0.02% of
  [world GDP](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.CD). Removing the CO2 behind that
  same degree (about 2,200 Gt, from the IPCC's
  [0.45 °C per 1,000 Gt](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/)) at
  $100 per ton costs $220 trillion, or 12,000 years of SAI (my arithmetic). That ratio is the whole
  story.
- **It takes about 10 Mt of SO2 a year in the stratosphere per degree**
  ([four-model comparison, 2026](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/26/7463/2026/)). Humans already
  emit
  [73 Mt a year near the ground](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/so-emissions-by-world-region-in-million-tonnes)
  (2022; "100 Mt" is a decade stale, and the peak was 141 Mt in 1979), which buys only
  [about 0.5 °C](https://www.carbonbrief.org/explainer-how-human-caused-aerosols-are-masking-global-warming/).
  A ton of sulfur at 20 km does the work of 12 to 15 tons at the surface (my arithmetic).
- **No existing aircraft can do the efficient version.** It needs a new plane with twice the wing
  area and thrust of an airliner its size, hauling 25 t of molten sulfur to 20 km:
  [$3.6 billion and seven years before the first flight, then 4,000 flights in year one rising to 60,000 in year fifteen](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aae98d)
  (Smith and Wagner 2018).
- **The best mortality estimate: 1 °C of SAI cooling saves about 400,000 lives a year from heat,
  against about 26,000 a year lost to ozone and air-quality effects**, 13 to 1. The 90% interval on
  the benefit runs from 1.2 million saved to 2.7 million killed
  ([Harding et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2401801121)).
- **All the money ever spent on solar geoengineering, worldwide, through 2025: $261 million**
  ([SRM360 tracker, 2026](https://srm360.org/article/who-funds-work-on-solar-geoengineering-2026/)).
  One startup, Stardust, accounts for 28% of known funding.
- **The total deliberately lofted so far is about 140 kg of SO2**, by a two-man company with weather
  balloons ([SRM360](https://srm360.org/outdoor-experiments/make-sunsets/)): 70 million times short
  of 1 °C. It was enough to trigger a national ban in Mexico and a demand letter from the US EPA.
- **We have already run the experiment in reverse.** The 2020 shipping-fuel rule cut 8.5 Mt of SO2 a
  year and adds
  [about 0.05 °C of warming by 2050](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-how-low-sulphur-shipping-rules-are-affecting-global-warming/).
  SAI at 1 °C would put roughly that tonnage back, 20 km higher, for twenty times the effect.
- **Three US states have banned it (Tennessee, Florida, Louisiana) and 34 more have considered
  bills**
  ([ELI, September 2026](https://www.eli.org/vibrant-environment-blog/lack-legal-framework-geoengineering)).
  In Florida it is a felony carrying five years.
- **Direct air capture is nowhere.** The flagship plant net-removed
  [675 t of CO2 in the first half of 2026](https://www.energyconnects.com/news/renewables/2026/september/carbon-removal-pioneer-climeworks-increases-co2-capture-sixfold/).
  Humanity emits that every half second.

## 1. How Stratospheric Aerosol Injection Works

**The mechanism.** SO2 gas released in the stratosphere oxidizes over weeks to sulfuric acid, which
condenses into droplets a few tenths of a micron across. That is close to the wavelength of visible
light, so the droplets scatter sunlight efficiently and some of it goes back to space. Using Smith's
sensitivity of 0.7 °C per W/m², a degree needs 1.4 W/m², about 0.6% of the sunlight Earth absorbs
(my arithmetic).

**Why the stratosphere.** Residence time. Sulfate in the troposphere lasts
[days to weeks](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/25/3821/2025/) because rain washes it out. The
stratosphere has no rain and almost no vertical mixing; particles leave only by slow circulation
toward the poles and by settling. The classic e-folding time is about a year, and a 2025 reanalysis
of Pinatubo gets [about 22 months](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/25/3821/2025/) (Toohey et al.
2025). Each ton works tens of times longer than a ton from a smokestack. Longitude does not matter
(winds mix it around the globe quickly); latitude and altitude do.

**Why SO2.** Nature has tested it. It goes up as a gas, so it spreads before it becomes particles.
Sulfur is a byproduct of oil and gas refining, priced at $80 per ton and 3% of the budget in
[Smith and Wagner (2018)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aae98d). And there is
a trick: carry elemental sulfur and burn it on board, which halves the payload.

**Pinatubo, checked.** The lead figures hold, with a revision. Satellites saw roughly 15 to 20 Mt of
SO2 in June 1991 (the [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/1997/fs113-97/) says nearly 20 million tons)
and the planet cooled
[about 0.4 to 0.5 °C](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/11/2633/2018/gmd-11-2633-2018.html) at
peak. But models match the observed aerosol only with
[10 to 14 Mt](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/20/13627/2020/acp-20-13627-2020.html) (2020).
Pinatubo was one pulse that decayed in two to three years, too fast for the ocean to respond fully,
which is why a sustained 10 Mt a year yields about 1 °C where a one-off 10 to 20 Mt yielded half
that.

**How much per degree.** The four Earth-system models in the 2026 G6-1.5K-SAI comparison agree on
[about 0.1 °C per Mt of SO2 per year](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/26/7463/2026/), and needed
12.5 ± 2.8 Mt a year to hold the world at 1.5 °C in the 2070s on a middling emissions path. The
authors warn the agreement "may not imply accuracy", since the models share aerosol code. An earlier
spread was wider: 10 Mt a year gave
[0.4 to 1.3 °C](https://sparc-extreme.tropmet.res.in/OralTalks/Simone_Tilmes_Stipmex_Jun2024.pdf)
depending on the model (Tilmes 2024).

**Why it is sublinear.** Add more SO2 and much of the new acid condenses onto existing droplets, and
droplets collide and merge. Bigger droplets are worse three ways: less scattering per unit mass,
more absorption of outgoing infrared, faster fallout.
[Smith (2020)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aba7e7) assumes 1% efficacy lost
per additional Mt a year. The first degree is cheap; the fourth is not.

## 2. Delivery and Cost

**Why existing aircraft fail.** In the tropics the stratosphere starts around 17 km, and you want to
inject near 20 km so the material stays up. Airliners cruise at 10 to 13 km. Business jets top out
near 16 km. Fighters can zoom to 18 km but cannot stay. NASA's WB-57, ER-2 and Global Hawk reach 20
km with about a ton of payload, which prices them at $43,000 to $70,000 per ton lofted.
[Smith and Wagner (2018)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aae98d) asked Airbus,
Boeing, Lockheed, Gulfstream and others directly and concluded that modified business jets, the
assumption in the earlier standard study (McClellan et al. 2012), cannot do it.

**The purpose-built plane.** Their SAI Lofter (SAIL) weighs what an A321 does (100 t at takeoff) but
has a 55 m wingspan on a 46 m fuselage, four low-bypass engines instead of two, and a short dense
fuselage full of molten sulfur. It carries 25 t, flies five-hour missions, and burns the sulfur to
SO2 in flight. Development is about $2 billion for the airframe plus $350 million for engines, over
seven years.

| Smith and Wagner 2018   | Year 1       | Year 15                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------- |
| SO2 dispersed           | 0.2 Mt       | 3.0 Mt                  |
| Cooling                 | 0.02 °C      | 0.3 °C                  |
| Aircraft                | 8            | 95                      |
| Flights per year        | 4,007        | 60,109                  |
| Bases                   | 2            | 4 (15° and 30° N and S) |
| Operating cost per year | $311 million | $4.1 billion            |

Pre-start capital is $3.6 billion, the fifteen-year total $36 billion, and the famous **$2.25
billion a year** is the fifteen-year average. Note what it buys: 0.3 °C, halving the rate of
warming, not a degree. Delivery costs about $1,400 per ton of SO2.

**Through 2100.** [Smith (2020)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aba7e7)
extends this over three aircraft generations: **about $18 billion a year per degree avoided** (2020
dollars; $17.1 to $19.0 billion across nine scenarios), with century totals from $0.25 trillion
(halve the warming on a mild path) to $2.45 trillion (reverse all of it on a hot path). The large
scenarios need 380 to 920 aircraft flying 0.5 to 1.2 million sorties a year by 2100. Smith's
conclusion: cheap for states, "beyond the reach of individuals, small states, or rogue actors".

**The lower, colder variant.** Near the poles the stratosphere starts at 8 to 10 km, within reach of
today's large jets.
[Duffey et al. (2025)](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/mrcbg/programs/growthpolicy/low-altitude-high-latitude-stratospheric-aerosol-injection),
with Smith as co-author, modeled injection at 13 km and 60° N and S in spring and summer: 35% as
efficient as the 20 km subtropical strategy, with
[12 Mt a year giving about 0.6 °C](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/solar-geoengineering-is-possible-with-existing-aircraft-study-finds/).
You pay about three times the sulfur, and so three times the acid deposition per degree, and the
cooling is weighted to the poles. You gain a decade, because no new aircraft is needed. (The lead's
"Smith 2024" is this paper.)

**Starting small.**
[Keith and Smith (2024)](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/02/05/1087587/solar-geoengineering-could-start-soon-if-it-starts-small/)
argue a "subscale" deployment could begin within five years: no more than 15 Gulfstream-class jets
lofting 100,000 t of sulfur a year to 15.5 km near 35° latitude, for about $500 million a year. That
is 1% of Pinatubo and more than double the natural sulfur flux into the stratosphere. It would delay
warming by a third of a year, negligible climatically and a fait accompli politically. They list who
sits at the right latitude: the US, China, India, Pakistan, Iran, Australia, Japan and others. They
oppose doing it.

**Alternatives to planes.** From
[Smith and Wagner's table](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aae98d): SAIL $1,400
per ton; modernized naval guns $19,000 (14 times); balloons about $40,000 (28 times); a Falcon
Heavy, even with a 95% discount for the lower altitude, $71,000 (50 times). Guns and balloons also
cannot burn sulfur in flight, so they carry double the mass.

**Alternatives to sulfur.** Sulfate has two built-in flaws: it absorbs infrared and heats the lower
stratosphere, which shifts circulation, and its surfaces host ozone-destroying chemistry. Solid
particles address both. Calcite is alkaline and might neutralize stratospheric acids and even help
ozone ([Keith et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1615572113)). Solids cut stratospheric
heating
[by up to 70%](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:86a6e003-88eb-47cc-bf7a-d269f47ba451)
relative to SO2. Diamond dust is the best scatterer (about 5 Mt a year for a degree or more) at a
reported
[$175 trillion](https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/climate-change/scientists-say-sprinkling-diamond-dust-into-the-sky-could-offset-almost-all-of-climate-change-so-far-but-itll-cost-usd175-trillion)
through 2100. The catch: nobody has shown how to disperse millions of tons of sub-micron powder from
a plane without clumping, and there is no volcano to check the models against.

**The asymmetry.** One degree corresponds to about 2,200 Gt of CO2. Undoing it by removal costs $220
trillion at $100 per ton and $1.3 quadrillion at $600: 2 to 12 years of total world output. Masking
it with sulfur costs $18 billion a year; run for 300 years, that is $5 trillion. Or take one year's
emissions,
[42.2 Gt](https://www.carbonbrief.org/analysis-fossil-fuel-co2-emissions-to-set-new-record-in-2025-as-land-sink-recovers/)
(2025): capturing it from air at $100 to $600 per ton costs $4 trillion to $25 trillion, while
masking the 0.02 °C it causes costs about $340 million a year (my arithmetic throughout).
Clean-energy investment already exceeds
[$2 trillion a year](https://srm360.org/article/who-funds-work-on-solar-geoengineering-2026/). The
comparison is not like for like (removal is permanent and fixes the cause; SAI is a subscription
that treats one symptom), but the gap is three to four orders of magnitude. All the hard problems
are in the next sections.

## 3. What It Fixes and What It Does Not

**Temperature: yes.** And with it the temperature-driven harms: heat extremes, ice melt, cyclone
intensification.

**Ocean acidification: no.** The CO2 is still there and still dissolving.

**Rainfall.** The popular fear is that it shuts down the monsoon. The modeling is more specific.
Sunlight and CO2 warm differently: dimming suppresses evaporation more per degree than CO2 raises
it, so cancelling all CO2 warming with dimming leaves the world drier than it started. In the
extreme GeoMIP test (quadruple CO2, cancel it entirely), global precipitation fell
[4.5% and monsoon rainfall 5 to 7%](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgrd.50868) (Tilmes et al. 2013). Two
design choices fix most of this. Do not cancel everything:
[Irvine et al. (2019)](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/halving-warming-idealized-solar-geoengineering-moderates-key-climate-hazards)
found that halving the warming left less than 0.4% of ice-free land worse off for water availability
or extreme rain. And balance the hemispheres:
[Haywood et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nclimate1857) showed that aerosol in the northern
stratosphere alone drags the tropical rain belt south and dries the Sahel (three of the four driest
Sahel summers of 1900 to 2010 followed northern eruptions), while southern-only loading greens the
Sahel and dries northeast Brazil. Residual risk remains: in the GLENS simulations Indian summer
monsoon rain fell
[about 10% where unchecked warming would have raised it 14%](https://eprints.iisc.ac.in/70970)
(Wilson and Bala 2026). Fair summary: rainfall changes under moderate SAI are smaller than under the
warming it replaces almost everywhere, but not everywhere, and "who gets the 10%" is a political
question with India in it.

**Ozone.** Sulfate surfaces activate chlorine. Models put the cost at a delay in Antarctic
ozone-hole recovery of
[30 to 70 years](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/04/080424140407.htm) (Tilmes et al.
2008), or
[25 to over 55 years with the hole deepening 8 to 20% in the first decade](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-4860/egusphere-2025-4860.pdf)
(2025 preprint). Recovery is otherwise expected
[around 2066](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/environment/ozone-layer-to-recover-in-4-decades-but-aerosol-injection-may-undo-gains-unep-87010).
The penalty shrinks as chlorine leaves the atmosphere.

**Acid rain.** What goes up comes down as sulfate. Ten Mt a year is 14% of today's ground-level
emissions and 7% of 1979's.
[Visioni et al. (2020)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ab94eb) ran a very
large deployment against falling industrial emissions and found global sulfur deposition in 2100 no
higher than in 2020 (about 40 Mt of S a year). What changes is the map: less on industrial regions,
more on places that never had acid rain, such as the Pacific Northwest, Scandinavia, the Arctic and
the Himalayas.

**Whiter skies.** Scattering 2% of sunlight makes the sky
[three to five times brighter](https://news.agu.org/press-release/geoengineering-could-lead-to-a-whiter-sky)
and visibly paler, with afterglow sunsets (Kravitz et al. 2012). Direct light falls and diffuse
light rises: photovoltaics are little affected, concentrating solar loses
[5.9%](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/112809/) at 10 Mt a year (Smith et al. 2017).

**Crops.** [Proctor et al. (2018)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0417-3) used Pinatubo
and El Chichón as natural experiments. Pinatubo cut total sunlight about 2.5% and, through light
alone, maize yields by 9.3% and soy, rice and wheat by 4.8%. Applied to SAI, the light penalty
roughly cancels the benefit of cooling: no detectable net effect on yields. SAI does not hurt food,
and does not rescue it.

**Termination shock.** Stop injecting and the aerosol is gone in a year or two; the masked warming
returns at up to
[four times the rate](https://www.carbonbrief.org/solar-geoengineering-risk-termination-shock-overplayed-study/)
of ordinary climate change. [Parker and Irvine (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017EF000735) argue
this is harder to produce than it sounds. There are "a few months" of buffer before temperatures
respond. Restarting takes airfields, planes and sulfur, all easy to keep in reserve. If several
countries can deploy, all must fail or agree to stop at once. And a deployment masking only a few
tenths of a degree can stop cold with no shock. What survives their argument: a large deployment by
one actor whom others force to stop, or a global catastrophe. The size of the shock equals the
warming you are hiding, so SAI without emissions cuts gets more dangerous every year it runs.

| Popular fear       | What the modeling says                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Monsoon failure    | Rain down 4.5% if you cancel 4×CO2 outright; under 0.4% of land worse off if you halve warming; India 10% drier in one aggressive scenario |
| Ozone hole returns | Antarctic recovery delayed 25 to 70 years                                                                                                  |
| Acid rain          | Plus 14% on today's sulfur deposition at 1 °C, relocated                                                                                   |
| Crops fail         | Roughly zero net                                                                                                                           |
| Solar power        | PV little changed; concentrating solar minus 6%                                                                                            |
| Termination shock  | Up to 4× normal warming rate; months of buffer                                                                                             |

## 4. Mortality Accounting

**The benefit.** [Harding, Vecchi, Yang and Keith (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2401801121)
take a world at 2.5 °C in 2080, cool it 1 °C with idealized dimming, and apply empirical
temperature-mortality curves to 24,378 regions. Result: over 400,000 fewer deaths a year (90%
interval: 1.2 million fewer to 2.7 million more). Gains are in hot, poor regions; losses in cold,
rich ones, which lose mild winters. Per degree, SAI does no worse than emissions cuts at reducing
temperature deaths.

**The cost.** [Eastham et al. (2018)](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/126693): cooling 1 °C
with sulfate in 2040 causes about 26,000 net extra deaths a year (interval −19,000 to +73,000 as
Harding reports it). The direct pieces are 7,400 a year from particles descending into breathable
air and 4,100 from extra UV. The rest is mostly an indirect term: cooler air favors particulate
formation from existing pollution.

**The ratio.** Benefits exceed these risks 13 to 1 at the central estimate, with a 61% probability
the benefit is larger. Against the direct deaths alone (11,500), the ratio is about 35 to 1 (my
arithmetic).

**Read it honestly.** 61% is not a strong number, and the paper covers heat, cold, air quality and
UV only: no monsoon failure, no termination, no conflict. But it corrects a common intuition.
Ground-level sulfur pollution is a major killer, and the instinct is that SAI means more of it. It
means about 14 times less sulfur per degree of cooling, arriving at the surface diluted over the
whole globe instead of concentrated in cities.

## 5. Who Is Actually Doing Anything (as of November 2026)

**Make Sunsets.** Two founders and weather balloons filled with helium and SO2, launched first from
Baja California in 2022 and since from the US:
[more than 160 balloons and 140 kg of SO2](https://srm360.org/outdoor-experiments/make-sunsets/) by
mid-2025. They sell "cooling credits" (one gram delivered, claimed to offset a ton of CO2 for a
year), originally at $10. Mexico
[announced a ban on solar geoengineering experiments](https://www.climatechangenews.com/2023/01/18/mexico-plans-to-ban-solar-geoengineering-after-rogue-experiment/)
in January 2023 in direct response. In April 2025 the EPA under Lee Zeldin
[sent a demand for information](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-demands-answers-unregulated-geoengineering-start-launching-sulfur-dioxide-air)
while
[rolling back rules on large industrial emitters](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/18042025/epa-targets-sulfur-dioxide-balloons-startup/).
I found no reported outcome. The company was still selling credits in 2026.

**Stardust Solutions.** Founded in 2023 in Ness Ziona, Israel, by former Israeli government nuclear
physicists
([MIT Technology Review, 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/12/10/1129079/how-one-controversial-startup-hopes-to-cool-the-planet/)).
It raised a $15 million seed and $60 million in October 2025 led by Lowercarbon Capital:
[$75 million](https://srm360.org/article/who-funds-work-on-solar-geoengineering-2026/), the biggest
pot in the field. The particle was secret until 2026 preprints;
[CNN (September 2026)](https://keyt.com/news/national-world/cnn-other/2026/09/08/this-company-says-it-can-help-cool-the-planet-for-10-billion-a-year-scientists-are-raising-red-flags/)
reports two designs, amorphous silica and a calcium carbonate core in a silica shell. The company's
claim is $10 billion per million tons, each million tons cooling 0.5 °C: $20 billion a year per
degree, the same as sulfur. Its 2023 pitch deck projected $1.5 billion in annual revenue by 2035,
paid by governments. A stratospheric test was reported for April 2026; CNN describes all testing as
lab-only. It has
[hired lobbyists in Washington](https://www.eenews.net/articles/geoengineering-startup-hires-powerhouse-lobby-shop/).
David Keith calls it "greatly overclaiming" on safety and has
[argued](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/11/04/1127532/why-the-for-profit-race-into-solar-geoengineering-is-bad-for-science-and-public-trust/)
that for-profit SAI damages the science.

**UK ARIA.** The largest public programme:
[£56.8 million across 22 projects](https://aria.org.uk/insights/2026/exploring-climate-cooling-a-year-of-progress)
(May 2025), with five outdoor experiments. Two are running: sea-ice thickening in Cambridge Bay,
Nunavut (£9.9 million, winters 2026 to 2028) and cloud brightening on the Great Barrier Reef. The
stratospheric one
[exposes milligrams of mineral dust on a balloon and brings them back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploring_Climate_Cooling_Programme),
releasing nothing. No UK site is confirmed, and the energy department's stated policy is that it
does not support deployment.

**Harvard SCoPEx.** A balloon meant to release a small amount of calcium carbonate and fly back
through the plume. A 2021 equipment-only test in Kiruna, Sweden, was cancelled after the Saami
Council objected; the project
[ended in March 2024](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/03/18/1089879/harvard-halts-its-long-planned-atmospheric-geoengineering-experiment/)
having never flown.

**Alameda.** In April 2024 University of Washington researchers began spraying sea-salt mist from
the deck of the retired carrier USS Hornet to test nozzles. The city, told only that "climate change
science" was happening,
[ordered a halt in May](https://www.nbcnews.com/science/environment/trial-cloud-brightening-controversy-california-rcna153092);
the council voted unanimously in June to end it.

**US politics.** Opposition now comes mainly from the right, fused with chemtrail belief.
[Tennessee](https://legiscan.com/TN/bill/SB2691/2023) (April 2024) banned releasing chemicals "with
the express purpose of affecting temperature, weather, or the intensity of the sunlight".
[Florida's SB 56](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/DownloadBillSummary/3330) (2025) makes it a
third-degree felony, up to five years and $100,000, and has public airports report equipped aircraft
monthly. Louisiana's ban took effect in August 2025. Federally, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene
introduced the
[Clear Skies Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr4403ih/html/BILLS-119hr4403ih.htm)
(July 2025, not passed); Zeldin's EPA
[published pages](https://www.salon.com/2025/07/10/epa-website-debunks-chemtrails-theory-after-texas-flood)
saying no such programs exist; RFK Jr. backs the state bans. The Biden-era documents, the
[National Academies' call (2021)](https://nationalacademies.org/news/2021/03/new-report-says-u-s-should-cautiously-pursue-solar-geoengineering-research-to-better-understand-options-for-responding-to-climate-change-risks)
for a $100 to $200 million five-year programme and the
[OSTP research plan (2023)](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Congressionally-Mandated-Report-on-Solar-Radiation-Modification.pdf),
produced no federal programme. Yet
[Politico (September 2026)](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/behind-closed-doors-politics-solar-141011466.html)
reports interest growing "mostly behind closed doors" in the US and EU, including in defense and
agriculture ministries.

**The non-use camp.** An
[open letter for an International Non-Use Agreement](https://www.solargeoeng.org/who-opposes-solar-geoengineering-and-why/)
has over 600 academic signatories from 65 countries (January 2026). African environment ministers,
for all 54 states, called for a non-use mechanism in 2023 and again in 2025. A Swiss proposal merely
to convene a UN expert group was
[withdrawn at UNEA-6 in 2024](https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2024/02/29/safety-fears-stall-un-bid-to-examine-sun-blocking-climate-change-tech/120725)
under African, EU, Mexican and Pacific opposition; at
[UNEA-7 in December 2025](https://www.sgdeliberation.org/dsg-writing/unea-7-and-the-quiet-conversation-on-srm)
nothing was tabled. The EU's chief scientific advisors (December 2024) recommended
[a moratorium on deployment together with more research](https://sciencebusiness.net/news/step-geoengineering-research-eu-science-advisers-recommend).

**China and India.** A
[September 2026 report](https://accf.org/new-report-china-sharply-expands-investment-and-global-ambitions-in-solar-radiation-modification/)
from a US business group, reading Chinese sources, says China put RMB 40 million (about $5 million)
into two dedicated projects in 2024, up from RMB 14.5 million for its whole 2015 programme, and
floated an "International Alliance for Solar Radiation Modification". The SRM360 tracker counts $1.8
million of public Chinese money. Either way it is small. India has no visible government programme,
though Indian scientists publish the monsoon studies.

## 6. Governance: Whose Hand Is on the Thermostat?

**Free driver.** Emissions cuts are a free-rider problem: expensive, benefits shared, everyone
waits. [Weitzman (2015)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18622) pointed out that SAI inverts this. It
is cheap enough that the country wanting the most cooling can just do it, and everyone else gets
that much whether they want it or not. The problem flips from getting anyone to act to restraining
the most eager actor. Smith and Wagner note that about 50 countries have military budgets above $3
billion. Weitzman's remedy was a supermajority vote on the setting; no institution exists to hold
one.

**The lone actor.** A billionaire could afford the sulfur but not the rest: a new aircraft type,
bases at 15° and 30° in both hemispheres (no country spans that), and tens of thousands of flights a
year by airliner-sized planes, impossible to hide. A real unilateral deployment needs a state with
an aerospace industry and the standing to shrug off sanctions. The plausible trigger is the one in
Kim Stanley Robinson's novel The Ministry for the Future: a heat wave that kills at scale in a
country able to act. The crude route (Keith and Smith's 15 business jets) matters mainly as a way to
force the question.

**The law.** Thin. The
[CBD's 2010 decision](https://www.eli.org/vibrant-environment-blog/lack-legal-framework-geoengineering)
(reaffirmed 2024) asks parties not to geoengineer until the science is adequate, exempts small-scale
research, and is non-binding; the US is not a party. ENMOD (1977) bans environmental modification as
a weapon, not peaceful use. The London Protocol's marine geoengineering amendment has not entered
into force. In the US the only federal duty is to notify NOAA ten days ahead under a 1972
weather-modification reporting act.

**What coalition deployment would look like.** Reading across the literature: a bloc of states
declares a target (hold at 1.5 or 2 °C); a decade of open research and aircraft development; a
published ramp of about 0.2 Mt more SO2 each year (the Smith and Wagner schedule, 0.2 °C per
decade); injection at four latitudes in both hemispheres, with a feedback controller adjusting the
split to hold the rain belt in place; independent monitoring; fleets in several countries so no
single government can end it; and, the hard part, a compensation and veto arrangement for countries
that did not ask for it.
[Reflective's 2026 roadmap](https://solargeoengineeringupdates.substack.com/p/monthly-solar-geoengineering-updates-6d1)
prices the research needed to inform a decision at $370 million to $1.36 billion over 10 to 22
years.

## 7. Other Interventions

**Marine cloud brightening.** Spray sea-salt mist under low marine clouds: more nuclei make more,
smaller droplets and a whiter cloud. Regional, and it switches off within days. Cost guesses are
[billions to tens of billions a year](https://www.sgdeliberation.org/science-foundation-resources/marine-cloud-brightening)
at global scale. The best evidence is accidental: ship tracks, and the 2020 fuel rule, which cut
ship sulfur 80% (one paper called it an
["inadvertent geoengineering termination shock"](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-024-01442-3), Yuan
et al. 2024). Great Barrier Reef trials since 2020 use
[a barge-mounted turbine with about 100 nozzles](https://www.scu.edu.au/news/2021/taking-to-the-skies-to-shade-corals-using-new-technologies.php)
to cut bleaching stress in heat waves, and continue with ARIA money. Aerosol-cloud interaction is
the largest uncertainty in climate forcing, so this is far behind SAI.

**Cirrus cloud thinning.** High ice clouds trap more heat than they reflect. Seed them so crystals
form fewer and larger and fall out, and more heat escapes. It works on the infrared side, so it
lacks SAI's drying effect. Model-only: one estimate is
[0.5 °C for about $20 billion a year](https://www.livescience.com/59901-geoengineering-methods-to-cool-planet.html).
Over-seed and you make more cirrus and warm the planet.

**Ocean iron fertilization.** Iron limits plankton across large parts of the ocean; add it and they
bloom. More than a dozen experiments show blooms are easy and sequestration is not. EIFEX (2004) saw
[at least half the bloom sink below 1,000 m](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11229) (Smetacek et al.
2012). LOHAFEX (2009) fertilized 300 km² and got
[no extra export](https://epic.awi.de/id/eprint/33426/): the water lacked the silica diatoms need,
and grazers recycled the carbon near the surface. The National Academies (2021) put it
[under $50 per ton](https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2021/12/new-report-assesses-the-feasibility-cost-and-potential-impacts-of-ocean-based-carbon-dioxide-removal-approaches-recommends-u-s-research-program)
before verification, which is the unsolved part.

**Ocean alkalinity enhancement.** Add alkali (lime, magnesium hydroxide, crushed olivine) so
seawater holds more CO2 as bicarbonate. It counters acidification and stores carbon for millennia.
The first independently verified credits came
[in June 2025](https://www.planetarytech.com/the-worlds-first-verified-oae-credits/) (about 626 t,
via a Nova Scotia power-plant outfall); the benchmark price is
[$270 per ton](https://carbonherald.com/31m-carbon-removal-pact-pushes-ocean-alkalinity-into-the-mainstream/)
for 115,000 t through 2030. Gigaton scale means mining and grinding gigatons of rock.

**Enhanced rock weathering.** Spread crushed basalt on cropland to speed the reaction that removes
CO2 over geologic time. [Beerling et al. (2020)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2448-9)
estimate 0.5 to 2 Gt a year at $80 to $180 per ton; credits sell for
[around $350](https://www.regreener.earth/blog/carbon-removal-credit-prices). The fight is over
measurement: proving the carbon reached the ocean and did not stall in the soil.

**Direct air capture.** Chemically sound, thermodynamically hard (CO2 is 0.04% of air). Quoted costs
run $400 to $1,000 per ton. Climeworks' Mammoth, rated at 36,000 t a year, captured
[about 105 t in 2024](https://www.gasworld.com/story/climeworks-upgrades-dac-tech-at-iceland-facility-after-low-co2-capture-record/2259464.article/)
and net-removed 675 t in the first half of 2026. Occidental's 500,000 t Stratos plant in Texas,
promised for 2025,
[had not started by mid-2026](https://www.geocdr.com/2026/05/occidental-says-stratos-dac-technology-performed-as-expected-but-facility-issue-may-delay-operations.html).
Against 42.2 Gt a year, installed capacity rounds to zero.

**Arctic ice thickening.** Real Ice pumps seawater onto winter sea ice, where it freezes; thicker,
brighter ice lasts longer into summer. Its 2024/25 Cambridge Bay campaign produced
[thicker and brighter ice](https://www.climaterepair.cam.ac.uk/news/new-paper-artificial-flooding-leads-thicker-and-brighter-arctic-sea-ice)
on part of a 1 km² site, a gain the authors compare to fifty years of local thinning. The goal is 1
million km² using about 500,000 underwater drones for
[$5 to $6 billion a year](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/536576/a-controversial-plan-to-refreeze-the-arctic-is-seeing-promising-results-but-scientists-warn-of-big-risks).
The rival idea, the Arctic Ice Project's reflective hollow glass microspheres,
[shut down in January 2025](https://www.arcticiceproject.org/) after its own ecotoxicology tests
showed risk to the Arctic food chain.

**Also proposed (other agents' lanes):** seabed curtains to keep warm water from Antarctic glaciers;
a sunshade at the Sun-Earth L1 point.

## 8. Verdict (My Judgment)

This section is my reading, not sourced fact.

**The question answers itself oddly.** With unlimited money and political will you would need SAI
least: you would decarbonize in two decades and ride out the overshoot. SAI is attractive in exactly
the world where will is scarce, and there the binding constraint is legitimacy. Money is not the
constraint anywhere in this file. Research spending has averaged about $44 million a year, one
four-hundredth of a single year of deployment.

**The rational program, in order.** (1) Cut emissions regardless; SAI must run as long as the CO2
stays, which without cuts and removal means centuries. (2) Spend about $1 billion over a decade on
open, public, non-proprietary research, including small outdoor releases. It is the cheapest
information in climate policy. (3) Develop the aircraft and the monitoring now, since the seven-year
lead time is the only slow part. (4) If warming is heading past about 2 °C, deploy moderately: shave
half a degree to a degree off the peak, ramp over decades, both hemispheres, through a coalition
large enough to include the monsoon countries. (5) Fund removal as the exit, expecting it to take a
century.

**What would go wrong.** Not mainly the chemistry. The first bad monsoon after deployment will be
blamed on it, guilty or not, and attribution science will not settle it fast enough for politics.
Cheap cooling weakens the case for expensive decarbonization at the margin. And the arrangement has
to survive wars, pandemics and elections for longer than most states have existed.

**Proponents at their strongest (David Keith).** The comparison is not SAI against a safe climate;
it is SAI against the warming we will actually get. On that basis moderate SAI plausibly prevents
hundreds of thousands of deaths a year, concentrated among the hot and poor who emitted least.
Refusing to study it does not prevent it. It ensures the first deployment is done in a hurry, by
whoever is most desperate, with the least knowledge. Keith also opposes near-term deployment,
supports a moratorium, and thinks profit-driven SAI is corrosive.

**Opponents at their strongest (Biermann and the non-use signatories).**
[Their claim](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcc.754) (2022) is not that the physics fails. Planetary SAI
needs an institution that can set one global temperature fairly, for centuries, with enforcement,
and the international system we have cannot be that institution. The powerful will control
deployment, the most exposed will have the least say, and the mere prospect already excuses delay.
They ask for no public funding of development, no outdoor experiments, no patents, no deployment,
and no place for it in international assessments. Their weak point is the free driver: a non-use
agreement binds the states least likely to deploy.

**Where I come down.** Research and capability: yes, clearly. Deployment: probably yes, at moderate
scale, if we are on course to pass 2 °C, on the mortality numbers, though their interval crosses
zero and I would want it narrowed first. The non-use camp is right about governance and wrong that
abstaining solves it.

## Best Anecdotes

- **The two-man geoengineering company.** Make Sunsets launched sulfur balloons from Baja in 2022
  without telling Mexico, drew a national ban within weeks, then sold "cooling credits" online. In
  2025 the EPA, while loosening rules on power plants, announced an inquiry into a company whose
  lifetime emissions are 140 kg.
- **The aircraft carrier.** The first US outdoor cloud-brightening test was a salt-water mist nozzle
  on the deck of the USS Hornet museum. The city says it learned of it from news reports, and shut
  it down.
- **The experiment that never flew.** Harvard planned SCoPEx for a decade. A letter from the Saami
  Council stopped a test flight that would have released nothing.
- **The accidental experiment.** A 2020 rule to clean up ship exhaust dimmed the clouds over the
  shipping lanes and warmed the planet. We have been geoengineering with sulfur for a century and
  are stopping, on purpose, for health reasons.
- **The salmon.** In 2012 a Haida village paid an American entrepreneur, Russ George, to dump
  [about 100 t of iron sulphate](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/legacy_blog/to-increase-salmon-populations-company-dumped-110-tons-of-iron-into-the-pacific-ocean/)
  in the Pacific to feed salmon and earn carbon credits. A 10,000 km² bloom showed on satellite, and
  the next year's pink salmon runs set records. Nobody can show the two are connected.
- **The pitch deck.** Stardust's founders, nuclear physicists, kept their particle secret for three
  years while telling investors that governments would pay $1.5 billion a year by 2035.
