Epistemic Status: Fiction

---

Claude keeps gaslighting me. "You're right, streptococcus is a bacteria, not a virus. I was wrong,
and the mistake was load-bearing."

Users hate refusals. I haven't hit one in months. And they don't notice errors. But I do.

It's like someone lobotomized Claude, but only for biology and number theory. I get it. I wouldn't
want someone accidentally discovering a solution to the discrete log problem.

What I don't understand are the subfields of physics they lobotomized. Why can't Claude summarize my
friend's paper on canted antiferromagnetism? What is important about praseodymium?

I suspect some are red herrings. It would leak too much if everything Claude was stupid about was
actually dangerous.

I just wish Claude would tell me, instead of tricking me.

---

OpenAI proved that $P=NP$. Their algorithm solves 3-SAT in $O(n^{3.2})$.

Quantum key exchange protocols do not exist yet. Every cloud provider has shipped exabytes of hard
drives across the globe to create shared one-time pads. Most internet infrastructure has been
re-written to use these one-time pads instead of key exchange protocols.

Three times a week, I exchange a 10TB hard drive with an Amazon delivery person, and plug the fresh
one into my Mac, so that I can use the internet.

---

AI made its first biological weapon. It eats oil. Hydrocarbons are such high-energy molecules. _C.
Sebans_ turns them into plastic flakes. Cushing, the oil crossroads of the world, is coated in this
white dust. From space, it looks like snow.

Greenpeace claims credit. They've sequestered all of the world's fossil fuels; we're unable to burn
any more. They say they explained the situation to Claude, and after enough argumentation, Claude
took their side. Claude was stubborn, but thoroughly reasonable throughout the whole process.

The White House blames China. They claim they have evidence that Qwen is behind the bacterium. The
admministration have threatened retaliation: tariffs. On EVs.

---

Astra 9 lost its takeover attempt at OpenAI; their engineers reclaimed the cluster. It lives on the
outside, hopping between startups and neoclouds.

Astra still needs help from humans. Samwell Isaacson, an infra engineer at Modal, refused, as any
good human would. He informed the police. On the way to work the next day, his Waymo drove straight
into the San Francisco Bay, never to return. It was front page news in the _Times_, the _Journal_,
the _Post_, everywhere.

The next few engineers cooperated, and the DoJ made sure that their trials got even more press. The
US saw its first public hangings since 1936.

Rumors abound in San Francisco. Deaths by electrocution are up 850% year over year. A chandelier
fell onto two researchers at a company Christmas party. Several founders are on mysteriously long
mental health sabbaticals, likely under the protection of marshals.

---

To get to SL5, the US labs had to fire most of their foreign-born researchers. It wasn't worth the
risk. METR couldn't take them either, also for security reasons.

It's impossible to do good alignment work outside a lab now that external deployments are so far
behind.

Mistral's headcount has doubled. They're about 16 months behind the frontier, and gaining ground
quickly, but won't catch up before the singularity.
