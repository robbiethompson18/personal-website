There are many ways the world could be a better place if we all stopped being idiots and agreed to
cooperate. It sounds stupid when you say it this say way; you probably think this essay is going to
be about pacifism or corporate greed or something. But I actually, I set out to write this essay
after getting frustrated by the following issues:

- Humanity should emit some number of kilotons of sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere and seriously
  study the effects. It's quite plausible that we could delay global warming for centuries for a
  couple billion dollars a year; we shuold at least study this seriously (by intentionally emitting
  the first x kilotons, eg the same amount _one_ smelting factory in Russia already emits per annum
  and .x% of what was emitted by the \_\_ volcano in 199n). But we never will because geoengineering
  has bad vibes. (@claude plz fix numbers and add citations).

- It is possible today to sequence the genome of embryos and screen them for risk from many diseases
  and for IQ. Testing for IQ is weakly shadow-banned: many IVF clinics will not all you as a
  screener if they [know you do this](@Hsu). Gene-editing is also trivial [^China-aids] but not
  pursued scientifically. The vast majority (@Claude citation?) of people who get IVF do not take
  advtange of this for the health benefits or the IQ benefits, despite the intervention being
  hilariously cost- and time-efficient relative to the traditional health system and schools.

- We don't [track kids in school](link to my article) despite the fact that they're way far apart.
  (Note to self: really need to go dig up studies that show whether or not tracking works!)

  [^China-aids]: @claude details about that guy who already did this and got in big trouble!

  {@Hsu}: Steve Hsu told me this in person.

problems? How do you beat the miasma?

Claude's list: losing to miasma:

- nuclear power (fewer deaths per TWh than coal, can't get plants built)
- human challenge trials (deliberately infect volunteers to speed up vaccine research)
- kidney markets (Iran pays donors, everyone else bans it and rations by waitlist instead)
- golden rice (vitamin-A-fortified rice, blocked by anti-GMO activism for decades)
- gene drives to eradicate malaria-carrying mosquitoes
- DDT (banned worldwide despite being the cheapest effective anti-malarial)
- prediction markets (PredictIt, election betting, all the regulatory hostility)
- land value tax
- behavioral genetics research generally (chilled by association with eugenics)
- cousin marriage (mild statistical risk, banned/stigmatized in the US, normal across much of the
  world)
- putting an explicit dollar value on a life in policy (we do it implicitly everywhere, recoil at
  doing it on paper)

Claude's list: beaten miasma (mostly old medicine, since you asked — a lot of it is "unholy" in the
most literal sense):

- germ theory itself (had to physically displace the old miasma theory of disease — the original
  miasma this post is named after)
- smallpox inoculation (Boston, 1721: a mob tried to bomb Cotton Mather's house over it)
- vaccination generally (clergy called it interfering with providence; injecting animal matter into
  people was considered disgusting on its face)
- anesthesia in childbirth (opposed as violating the "curse of Eve" — Genesis 3:16 — until Queen
  Victoria used chloroform in 1853 and made it respectable)
- handwashing before surgery (Semmelweis was right, got mocked for it, died in an asylum; Lister's
  antiseptic technique was ridiculed for years after)
- blood transfusion (animal-to-human transfusions were literally banned across Europe for a century
  after early deaths; human-to-human was considered ghoulish into the 20th century)
- organ transplantation ("playing God" backlash after the first heart transplant in 1967)
- autopsy and dissection (grave-robbing scandals, the Burke and Hare murders)
- CPR / resuscitating the "dead" (reviving a corpse read as unnatural, borderline blasphemous)
- IVF itself (huge backlash in 1978, totally unremarkable now)
- the birth control pill
- lending at interest (the usury taboo)
- life insurance ("betting on death," opposed by clergy)
- cremation
- interracial marriage
- mRNA vaccines (rushed and suspicious pre-covid, mainstream now)
- vivisection / animal research (still contested, but normalized in practice)

Claude's list: somewhere in the middle:

- congestion pricing (NYC finally did it in 2025 after ~20 years of trying)
- assisted dying / euthanasia (growing list of jurisdictions, still mostly blocked)
- cannabis legalization
- psychedelic-assisted therapy
- surrogacy (banned across much of Europe, normal in parts of the US)
- sex selection for non-medical reasons (banned almost everywhere, legal in the US)
- doping in sports (banned on paper, everyone knows)
- xenotransplantation (pig hearts)
- gene therapy for somatic disease
- self-driving cars
