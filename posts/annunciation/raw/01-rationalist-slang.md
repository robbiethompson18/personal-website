# 01 — Rationalist slang and speech

Researched 2026-11-11. Verification tags: **[V]** = I opened the source this session and it says
this. **[S]** = confirmed only through a search-result snippet or a secondary summary. **[M]** =
from memory, not re-checked. **UNVERIFIED** = looked, could not confirm.

## 1. Most interesting findings

**1. Someone has described the "EA dialect" phonetically, and it includes your Ts.** Evolutionary
psychologist Geoffrey Miller, in a 2023 EA Forum comment: "many EAs adopt a sort of in-group 'EA
dialect' that involves very fast speaking rate, staccato pace (fast-pause-fast-pause), and slightly
over-annunciated consonants", which he reads "as some sort of IQ-signal". The post author (an
Italian EA) agreed that "IQ signalling is too many times more valued than trying to be clear and
socially aware". This is one informed observer's ear, not a measurement. [V]
https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/tZbMtxQk42teAFBYs/native-english-speaker-eas-could-you-please-speak-slower/comment/ZugFYNqT6xxGL8h4u
(2023)

**2. The mechanism was documented in a Bay Area high school in 1995-96.** Mary Bucholtz's
ethnography of self-identified nerds found they spoke "superstandard English": "lexical formality,
carefully articulated phonological forms, and prescriptively standard grammar". They resisted
"consonant-cluster simplification and the phonological reduction of unstressed vowels" (one kid,
"Erich", from the computer club, says full "going to" and "have to" where everyone says
gonna/hafta), used spelling pronunciations, and picked "Greco-Latinate" words over Germanic ones
("My observation is that..."). Her chain of inference: careful speech sounds like reading, reading
indexes intelligence, and "resisting phonological pressure" becomes iconic of "resisting peer
pressure". Those kids are about 46 now. [V] Bucholtz, "The Whiteness of Nerds: Superstandard English
and Racial Markedness", _Journal of Linguistic Anthropology_ 11(1):84-100 (2001).
https://escholarship.org/content/qt0663j2rg/qt0663j2rg_noSplash_cffeaf94c7c693646808c8f31f071bf8.pdf

**3. Released /t/ specifically is a studied variable, and it clusters in argumentative-intellectual
subcultures.** Eckert's 2008 review: hyperarticulated /t/ release shows up in "nerd girls, Orthodox
Jewish boys, and gay men". In Sarah Benor's 2001 study of an Orthodox school in northern California,
the leaders in /t/ release were boys who had studied Talmud in yeshiva, and they "were particularly
likely to release /t/ when making a strong point in an intellectual argument". Eckert's reading: it
"builds primarily on the social significance of clear speech", plus a British-equals-"cultured,
refined, and articulate" association. [V] Eckert, "Variation and the indexical field", _Journal of
Sociolinguistics_ 12(4):453-476 (2008).
https://web.stanford.edu/~eckert/Institute2011/Readings/eckert2008

**4. Rationalists don't know where their own words come from, and someone measured it.** A
preregistered 2018 survey of 53 LessWrongers asked whether each of 12 terms originated in academia
or in the LW diaspora. 77% got "Chinese Robber Fallacy" wrong and 64% got "motte and bailey" wrong
(it is from a 2005 philosophy paper); four terms were misattributed by a majority. The author's
worry: "In failing to transmit the sources of knowledge it's quite possible we're retarding progress
by making it non-obvious where to go for more." Small convenience sample from chatrooms. [V]
https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/no5Fc7Br8G2vCu24h/lesswrong-diaspora-jargon-survey (2018)

**5. "Epistemic status" descends from US intelligence-analysis vocabulary.** Lineage: the blogger
muflax tagged posts with an "epistemic state" ("I wanted a way to show whether I still believe
something I have written or not, and if so, how strongly"); gwern "stole the idea" and formalized it
with the Kesselman List of Estimative Words (Kesselman 2008, a thesis on the probability words in
National Intelligence Estimates [M for the thesis detail]: certain / highly likely / likely /
possible / unlikely / highly unlikely / remote / impossible); Scott Alexander loosened gwern's tags
into the freeform "Epistemic status:" line everyone copies. [V] https://gwern.net/about ; [V]
quanticle's 2018 comment at
https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Hrm59GdN2yDPWbtrd/feature-idea-epistemic-status. Scott's first use:
UNVERIFIED date.

**6. "Steelman" was not coined by the person usually credited.** The standard attribution is Chana
Messinger's December 2012 post. But lukeprog wrote on LessWrong in October 2011: "Sometimes the term
'steel man' is used to refer to a position's or argument's improved form", i.e. it was already
circulating. Word Spy's earliest hit is a one-off joke on the James Randi forum in 2003. Yudkowsky
rejects the practice (steelmanning "is not a tool of understanding and communication", comment on
Ozy's 2016 essay [S]), and a 2023 LW exchange (Rob Bensinger's "ITT-passing and civility are good;
'charity' is bad; steelmanning is niche" and a rebuttal post [S]) argued over whether the
Ideological Turing Test (Bryan Caplan, June 2011, a jab at Paul Krugman [S]) is the better norm. [V]
https://wordspy.com/words/steel-man

**7. The Sequences have a 1930s ancestor with the same habits.** Yudkowsky, 2011: "I'm explicitly
influenced by Korzybski on language." In 2020 he specified the route: "via 'Language in Thought and
Action' and the Null-A novels", i.e. Hayakawa's popularization and A. E. van Vogt's pulp SF, not
Korzybski's _Science and Sanity_ (1933). General Semantics also had a jargon-heavy self-improvement
community that claimed to be a science of sanity; the 2020 LW post that prompted Yudkowsky's reply
argues it decayed into dogma and spun off Scientology and NLP. "Taboo Your Words" (2008), by
contrast, is named after the Hasbro party game. [V]
https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/oTX2LXHqXqYg2u4g6/less-wrong-rationality-and-mainstream-philosophy/comment/899TGp3rNpenquY6D
(2011) ; [V] https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/r5jMMbW2yN9GzWXcR/time-binders-1 (2020)

**8. "Mesa-" was borrowed from a neuro-linguistic programming newsletter.** Hubinger et al. (2019)
call mesa-optimization "a neologism we introduce in this paper" and gloss it "whereas meta is Greek
for above, mesa is Greek for below", footnoted to Joe Cheal, "What is the opposite of meta?", _ANLP
Acuity_ vol. 2. ANLP is the Association for NLP [M]. The Greek gloss is loose: μέσα means
"inside/within", as an LW commenter pointed out [S]. So a staple prefix of alignment research traces
to NLP, which finding 7's source names as a General Semantics spin-off. [V]
https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.01820 (2019); Cheal bibliography entry [S].

**9. The spoken slang turns over fast; the forms persist.** Three time capsules: the 2012 "Shit
Rationalists Say" thread (top entries: "Is that your true rejection?", "I have akrasia", "One-box!",
"How do you operationalize that?", "What does the outside view say?", "I notice I am confused",
"Taboo 'monkey'"); the 2018 redo ("I thought Moloch had already eaten all the value there", "You may
have changed my betting odds, but you haven't changed my models!", "I consider myself
'rationalist-adjacent'", "I should make a TAP for that"); and a newcomer's 2026 Lighthaven field
notes on "Ratspeak" ("model", "updating priors", "agency", "-adjacent", "salient"). Akrasia,
one-boxing and true rejections are gone. "Operationalize", "outside view", "notice I'm confused",
"taboo X" and "model" survived 14 years. [V]
https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8xQ8hTxo6Rk2qqZfj/shit-rationalists-say (2012) ; [V]
https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/kNDLkcqLMcLdrQNzG/shit-rationalists-say-2018 ; [V]
https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JAR8b9G4NpNM6seTn/one-week-in-the-rat-farm (2026)

**10. The jargon drifts, and the coiners complain.** "Schelling point" (Schelling 1960: where people
converge _without_ communicating) drifted on LW to "any place they wanted to coordinate to meet at".
"Legible" (James C. Scott 1998: simplified so a state can control it) became "easy to verify at a
glance"; an EA wrote "we're using jargon because it makes us feel cool". Shackel says his
motte-and-bailey is a _doctrine_, not a fallacy: "Strictly speaking, neither is correct."
"Infohazard" drifted from Bostrom's "true information that enables harm" to "information that hurts
whoever holds it". [V]
https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/c882tMwMMSWQbWyBC/avoiding-jargon-confusion (2019) ; [V]
https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/pECcfcevpjJCbRLQJ/the-way-ea-uses-legible-is-not-very-legible
(2023) ; [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motte-and-bailey_fallacy

**11. "p(doom)" was coined by a non-researcher and took 14 years to break out.** Wikipedia:
"presumably first used by programmer Tim Tyler in a 2009 comment on LessWrong" (the evidence is
largely Tyler's own account; Futurism says the origin "isn't a matter of record"). Mainstream
arrival is datable: ABC Australia July 2023 ("It started as a dark in-joke"), Kevin Roose in the NYT
on 6 December 2023. Shoggoth is the cleanest case: @TetraspaceWest's tweet on 30 December 2022, NYT
feature on 30 May 2023, five months. e/acc: earliest reference 31 May 2022, named as a troll on
"EA". [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P(doom) ; [S] shoggoth and e/acc dates via
https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/shoggoth-with-smiley-face-artificial-intelligence and
https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cultures/eacc-effective-accelerationism

**12. Scott Alexander told rationalists to stop talking like this.** His 2019 style guide: use the
everyday word when it means the same thing. "utility" → happiness; "optimal" → best; "humans" →
people; "high probability of" → probably; "status" → respect; "rational" → reasonable; don't write
"70% chance" for general readers. "This will instantly make you sound 20% less like an evil robot."
"Never offend people with style when you can offend them with substance." [V]
https://slatestarcodex.com/2019/07/04/style-guide-not-sounding-like-an-evil-robot/ (2019)

**13. The "So, ..." sentence opener has a Silicon Valley origin story that a linguist checked.**
NYT, 21 May 2010 (Anand Giridharadas): the habit started with 1990s Valley engineers; Michael Lewis
wrote in 1999 that "When a computer programmer answers a question, he often begins with the word
'so.'" Mark Liberman ran it against the COHA corpus: sentence-initial "so" is rising, but as "a
rather gradual" long-run trend; the programmer origin is untested and there are examples back to
Chaucer. Verdict: real feature of tech speech, origin claim unsupported. [V]
https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=2570 (2010); NYT original [S], nytimes.com blocked my
fetch.

**14. LessWrong now ships an LLM to explain its own jargon.** JargonBot (1 November 2024)
auto-drafts hover glossaries for posts. Raemon's reason: "easier to read things get read more", so
dense posts lose. An institutional concession to a complaint going back to Jeff Kaufman's 2013
"Antijargon Project". The best defenses: Nate Soares ("the non-standard phraseology tracks a
non-standard way they're thinking"; "my internal dialect has drifted away from American English, and
that suits me just fine") and gwern (a weird coinage is memorable and searchable; plain words give
an illusion of transparency). The best attacks: gjm (it signals "bunch of weirdos with their own
jargon, avoid") and Logan Strohl (a handed-over term crowds out your own perception of the
distinction). [V] https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/sZvMLWrWomN28uWA9/jargonbot-beta-test (2024) ;
[V] Soares, c. 2021: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/4j9akHopJu97WMrgN/so8res-shortform-feed ; [V]
gjm and Strohl in the comments of
https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PCrTQDbciG4oLgmQ5/sapir-whorf-for-rationalists (2023) ; gwern in the
comments of the "Lizards" post in section 4 ; [V]
https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/R6coM4SgNTu5uwHLD/antijargon-project (2013)

### The spoken register: what sources attest

- **Phonetics:** fast, staccato, over-articulated consonants (Miller 2023). Careful articulation
  indexes literacy and independence (Bucholtz 2001). No formal study of rationalists.
- **Discourse moves (Sabien, "Basics of Rationalist Discourse", 2023 [V],
  https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/XPv4sYrKnPzeJASuk/basics-of-rationalist-discourse):** separate "I
  saw A" from "I think A implies B"; attach rough confidence aloud ("I'm sort of making up numbers
  here but it feels like it's less than ten percent likely?"); "Hang on—did you mean that
  literally?"; and for claims about other minds, "I'm noticing that I have a story about you", which
  is Circling / authentic-relating phrasing inside a rationalist norms document.
- **Directness:** Harker (2026): "No one at Lighthaven bullshits."
- **Hedging is contested internally.** One LW commenter called qualifiers "epistemic security
  theater" and was told their own comment began with "I think"
  (https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/rEKhZQNPpw2NgcdKu/should-we-use-qualifiers-in-speech, 2020
  [V]). Elo (2016): "be confident of weak effects, rather than unconfident of strong effects."
  Another commenter notes Yudkowsky and Scott barely hedge, so heavy hedging is a follower trait,
  not a founder trait [S].
- **Stratification:** Raemon's Magic: The Gathering analogy splits "common" jargon (must be
  memorable and hard to misread) from "expert" jargon, which "is only really useful if you're buying
  into a broader ecosystem of ideas" (2017 [V],
  https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DcRFTx62sTTRQo3Jw/common-vs-expert-jargon).

## 4. Dead ends / contested

- **No linguist has studied the rationalist dialect.** Searches for sociolinguistic, anthropological
  or corpus work on LessWrong or EA speech returned only insider writing. The nearest academic piece
  is STS: Ahmed, Jaźwińska, Ahlawat, Winecoff & Wang, "Field-building and the epistemic culture of
  AI safety", _First Monday_ 29 (2024), https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13626
  [V, abstract only]; a later paper says it lists shared jargon among the community's cohesion
  mechanisms [S]. In 2021 an EA Forum commenter proposed building a glossary by comparing EA Forum
  word frequencies to a general corpus [V]; I found no sign anyone did.
- **No LessWrong word-frequency time series found**, apart from one student RPubs word count of a
  single sequence.
- **"Rationalist accent":** nothing beyond Miller's comment and scattered anecdotes. Bucholtz and
  Eckert are about nerds generally. Harker's caveat applies throughout: much of this may be Bay Area
  tech speech.
- **Tim Tyler coining p(doom):** rests mostly on his own claim.
- **Circling vocabulary bleeding in:** CFAR's adoption of Circling is documented [S], and Sabien's
  guideline uses the phrasing, but I found no source tracing "I'm noticing..." or "what's alive for
  you" into rationalist speech. UNVERIFIED as a causal story.
- **"Lizardman's Constant":** a May 2026 LW post argues the term is actively misleading because
  bogus-response rates run 4-7% in opt-in polls and near zero in matched studies, so there is no
  constant; gwern defended the handle in the comments.
  https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/yqbsnjwgazrqNfwYf/lizards-and-less-wrong-jargon-a-brief-critique-of-convention
  [V]
- **"Steelman" in Merriam-Webster:** page returned 403; Dictionary.com's headword still only means
  "steelmaker" [S]. No data found on the mainstream spread of "update" / "priors". UNVERIFIED either
  way.
- **No canonical post titled "Use Normal Words".** Closest: Kaufman (2013), Aird (2020), "The Case
  for Reducing EA Jargon" (2021), Scott's style guide (2019).
